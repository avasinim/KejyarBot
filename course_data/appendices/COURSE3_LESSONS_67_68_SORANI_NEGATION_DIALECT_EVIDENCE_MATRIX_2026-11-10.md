# COURSE 3 — SORANI NEGATION: DIALECT-SCOPED EVIDENCE MATRIX
Date: 2026-11-10
Status: append-only scholarly research addendum for Lesson 68. This is an evidence map and proposed teaching structure, not specialist sign-off and not an approved runtime patch.

## Executive finding
The earlier shorthand "na- for present, ne- for past/subjunctive, me- for imperative" is useful only as a first approximation for a specified variety and construction. It is not a complete pan-Sorani rule. The evidence now in hand requires at least two separately labelled descriptions:

1. **Suleimaniya-oriented Sorani / standardized written description:** Haig (2018), drawing on MacKenzie and McCarus, distinguishes nā- negative indicative, ne- negative subjunctive, me- negative imperative, and ne- in selected past-tense contexts. The indicative prefix itself varies: e- in Suleimaniya/Wārmāwa and de- elsewhere, with e- used in the standard written variety.
2. **Ardalani Sorani (Sanandaj):** Tabei et al. (2024) report four negative markers: næ-, na:-, ni:-, mæ-. Their distribution and the behavior of continuous aspect/modal structures differ from a simple pan-Sorani template.

Ahmadi (2021) provides valuable formal rules and concrete past-progressive examples, but the manuscript itself says it was still being completed. Use it as a formal descriptive/preprint source, not as final specialist consensus. Do not collapse all the varieties into one universal conjugation chart.

## 2. Suleimaniya-oriented description — source-backed construction classes

### 2.1 Present stem: indicative, subjunctive and imperative

Haig's Table 6 gives the following examples with the present stem of «وتن» (wutin, ‘say’):

| Function | Prefix | Source example | Meaning | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Present indicative | e- (Suleimaniya/Wārmāwa); de- elsewhere | e-łē-m | I say / am saying | Source-backed; dialect variation explicit |
| Negative present indicative | nā- | nā-łē-m | I don't say | Source-backed |
| Present subjunctive | bi- | bi-łē-m | that I say | Source-backed |
| Negative present subjunctive | ne- | ne-łē-m | that I not say | Source-backed |
| Imperative, singular/plural | bi- | bi-łē / bi-łē-n | say! | Source-backed |
| Negative imperative | me- | me-łē / me-łē-n | don't say! | Source-backed |

Important teaching point: nā- and ne- are not interchangeable labels for "negative present". In this source's analysis, nā- marks negative indicative, while ne- marks negative subjunctive. The source also reports that imperative prefixes may be omitted in some environments; teach the cited examples, not an unrestricted formula.

### 2.2 Past tense and past imperfective

Haig reports a negative past example:

- min seg-eke=m ne-kušt — “I didn't kill the dog.”
- ne- may host/precede pronominal clitics in the verbal domain, as in min ne=m=kušt.
- The past imperfective in the Suleimaniya description is formed with the past stem plus e- (with dialectal variants); this is a distinct construction from the present indicative and must not be mechanically equated with a present progressive.

Ahmadi's formal rules add:
- Rule 7.2: ne- as the negative prefix in the simple past of intransitive verbs.
- Rule 7.3: negative transitive past forms with person/participant indexing and clitic ordering.
- These rules support teaching ne- in the cited past environments, but not a universal "all past verbs = ne + stem" rule without checking the verb class and agreement.

### 2.3 Past progressive — two separately described verb classes

Ahmadi Rules 7.9 and 7.10 provide examples for distinct classes:

| Rule | Verb class | Segmented source example | Translation | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7.9 | Intransitive | heł-ne-de-hat-în-ewe | we were not fleeing away | A particular verb/particle pattern; source manuscript is preliminary |
| 7.10 | Transitive | ne-yan-de-kêł-a | they were not ploughing | Clitic and transitivity structure differs; do not flatten into the intransitive rule |

These examples support the existence of ne- in certain past-progressive constructions. They do **not** prove that every Sorani verb, person, aspect or dialect forms past progressive negation by simply placing «نە» before «دە».

## 3. Ardalani Sorani — separate dialect profile

Tabei et al. (2024) analyze 263 negative sentences and affirmative counterparts from spoken Sorani broadcast material. Their article reports four negative markers in Ardalani:

| Marker (source transliteration) | Reported environment | Source-level summary |
|---|---|---|
| næ- | Past forms, subjunctive; also reported in aspectual structures | More productive/standard negative marker in the study |
| na:- | Present tense | Limited to present; in the cited analysis, the imperfective aspect affix is removed when na:- attaches |
| mæ- | Imperative | Replaces/deletes the affirmative imperative marker bə- in the example |
| ni:- | Present of «bûn» (‘be’) | Restricted negative form of the copular verb in the described Ardalani variety |

The study reports:
- Symmetric negation behavior in past perfective and past imperfective-habitual constructions.
- Asymmetric behavior in imperfective continuous aspect and modal structures.
- In continuous constructions, either finiteness/features move to an available copula, or aspect shifts from continuous to habitual.
- In modal structures, the modal marker is removed and replaced by the negative marker while modal meaning is retained.

The Ardalani findings are not to be silently imported into the Suleimaniya-standard table. The spellings/transliterations næ-, na:-, ni:-, mæ- are the source's analytical notation; do not automatically equate each one with a single Kurdish-script grapheme sequence across all orthographies.

## 4. Dialect variation — current safe synthesis

| Feature | Suleimaniya-oriented description (Haig 2018) | Ardalani (Tabei et al. 2024) | Teaching decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present indicative affirmative | e- in Suleimaniya/Wārmāwa; de- elsewhere | Study reports na:- as negative present; full positive prefix contrast should be sourced from its complete paradigm | Label variety; do not call e-/de- universal |
| Present indicative negative | nā- | na:- | Treat as related but dialect/notation-specific evidence; do not collapse without phonological/orthographic mapping |
| Present subjunctive negative | ne- | næ- appears in subjunctive environments | Keep mood and dialect labels |
| Negative imperative | me- | mæ- | Related function; use source's variety label |
| Past negative | ne- in examples/rules | næ- | Do not present one spelling/transliteration as universal |
| Negative copula | Negative predicate constructions require separate analysis | ni:- restricted to present «bûn» in the reported Ardalani description | Do not infer that every «نییە» in every Sorani variety is the same prefixal pattern |
| Past progressive negative | Ahmadi gives two selected rule patterns; Haig describes past imperfective separately | Continuous aspect may use a different asymmetric strategy | Keep specific examples and dialect source; no blanket productive rule |
| Modal negation | ne-/bi- paradigms described for subjunctive; details vary | Modal marker may be replaced by negator while modal meaning remains | Requires a separate advanced section |

## 5. Lesson 68 terminology and claims to revise

1. Replace the unqualified statement **“نا is present and future negation”** with: “In the Suleimaniya-oriented description, nā- marks negative present indicative; Sorani commonly uses present indicative forms in future contexts and does not have a dedicated future marker in Haig's account. Future interpretation is contextual, not a separate nā- future tense.”
2. Replace the unqualified statement **“نە is the past and present negator”** with construction-specific wording: “ne- marks negative subjunctive and appears in selected past constructions; the exact distribution depends on tense/aspect, verb class and variety.”
3. Keep **مە-** as the source-backed negative imperative marker in the Suleimaniya-oriented description; label the Ardalani cognate notation **mæ-** separately.
4. Teach **«نییە»** first as a negative predicate/copular expression in the course's complete sentence. Do not identify it automatically with the Ardalani prefix **ni:-** without a source-backed analysis of the exact form and variety.
5. Separate simple past, past imperfective, past progressive, present indicative, subjunctive, imperative and copular negation. Do not use “past continuous” as an umbrella for all past imperfective constructions.
6. Treat «نەدەنووسی» as an attested surface form only. Ahmadi's examples support selected patterns, but they do not directly establish the complete person/stem/aspect analysis of this exact word in the course's target variety.
7. Do not publish a six-person paradigm for «ناخوێنم» or «نەدەنووسی» from a single 1SG example. Require a dialect-labelled paradigm from a reliable grammar or specialist-checked elicitation.

## 6. Proposed replacement teaching sequence (not yet integrated)

### Lesson 68A — scope of negation
- Clause/verbal negation vs lexical negation.
- Negative verbal prefix vs negative copular predicate.
- Dialect and transcription labels.

### Lesson 68B — Suleimaniya-oriented present-stem constructions
- Negative indicative nā-.
- Negative subjunctive ne-.
- Negative imperative me-.
- Contrast each in full, cited sentences.

### Lesson 68C — past-stem constructions
- Simple past ne- examples, separating intransitive and transitive patterns.
- Past imperfective and past progressive as separate constructions.
- Ahmadi Rules 7.9/7.10 used as bounded examples, not a universal paradigm.

### Lesson 68D — Ardalani comparison
- næ-, na:-, ni:-, mæ- as the Ardalani article's inventory.
- Construction asymmetry in continuous and modal structures.
- Comparison table must not erase dialect differences.

### Lesson 68E — lexical negation and negative predicates
- «نییە» as a full predicate in sentence context.
- «بێسوود» and «تێرنەخۆر» as lexical items with evidence.
- No claim of free productivity from one or two dictionary words.
