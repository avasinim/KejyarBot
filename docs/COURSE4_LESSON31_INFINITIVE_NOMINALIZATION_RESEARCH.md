# Lesson 31 research note — Nominalization and syntactic uses of the Sorani infinitive

Date: 2026-11-07  
Course: KejyarBot Course 4  
Lesson: 31 — چاوگ لە ئەرکی ناویدا

## Scope

This lesson distinguishes the non-finite infinitive citation form from a nominalized verbal form used in a noun phrase or nominal syntactic position. It does not claim that every infinitive is an ordinary noun in every context.

## Source evidence

### 1. Kareem (2016), §4.5, pp. 112–114, example (29)

Rebeen Kareem analyzes the Central Kurdish nominalization example:

`kuşt-in-î gyandar-eke xrap bû` — “The killing of the animal was bad.”

The thesis describes nominalization as adding the infinitive/nominalizing suffix `-in` to the past stem. The nominalized form `kuştin` is linked by Ezafe `-î` to `gyandar-eke` (“the animal”). In Kareem's analysis, the animal is the Theme argument expressed in the larger nominal construction; this is not ordinary possessive ownership. The nominalized constituent functions as the subject of the clause.

### 2. Salavati & Ahmadi (2018), Figure 1, pp. 2–3

In the nominal group `nawendekanî dengdanman` (“our voting centers”), the paper identifies `dengdan` as a non-finite verb and `-man` as a possessive/person marker attached to it. This is direct evidence that a non-finite verbal form can occur in a nominal group and bear a nominal possessive marker.

## Analytical distinctions

1. **Infinitive vs nominalized infinitive:** the infinitive is a non-finite verbal form; nominalization is a use/construction in which the verbal form behaves as a nominal constituent. The two are related but not interchangeable labels.
2. **Nominal distribution and verbal argument structure:** nominalized forms can occupy nominal positions while retaining semantic participants associated with the underlying event. Ezafe may link the nominalized event to its Theme argument.
3. **Nominal markers:** possessive marking is attested on the non-finite form in Salavati & Ahmadi's example. Do not infer that every nominal inflection or every noun suffix can freely attach to every infinitive.
4. **Syntactic roles:** the Kareem example supports subject function; the Salavati & Ahmadi example supports nominal-group distribution and possessive marking. Other syntactic roles such as object and adpositional complement may be illustrated pedagogically, but require their own corpus or speaker evidence before being asserted as universal rules.
5. **Five nominal properties and seven syntactic functions:** no unified, authoritative closed list matching those exact counts was located in the sources reviewed. The lesson therefore does not invent or assert such a fixed taxonomy. It records the claim and its evidence limitation explicitly.
