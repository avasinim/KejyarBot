## Page-by-page findings

The Kurdish text discusses suffixation and word formation, with English examples/terms. This attribution is a separate reference; it should not be presented as the primary source's own original taxonomy or as direct evidence for every Kurdish form.
- **p.387:** The page continues the questions: **پێشگر (Prefix)**، **پاشگر (Suffix)**، and **ناوگر (Infix)**, including the explicit question whether an infix exists in Kurdish. It then introduces **نیشانە** and distinguishes two word groupings in the discussion. The question about Infix is a question raised by the text, not a conclusion that Kurdish has a productive infix category.
- **p.388:** A new major heading reads **وشەی سەربەخۆ و وشەی بەستراو**. The opening explanation discusses the need to distinguish independent words from bound forms in Kurdish grammatical description. Under **وشەی سەربەخۆ**, five classes are listed: **ناو، ڕاناو، ئاوەڵناو، کار (چاوگ)، ئاوەڵکار**. Preserve the parenthetical grouping **کار (چاوگ)** exactly as printed on this page.
- **p.389:** Completes the bound-word list with items 6–8: **ئامرازی پریپۆزیشن، ئامرازی عەتف، ئامرازی نیدا**. The page then restates the five independent classes and begins the section **دووەم — وشەی بەستراو (ناتەواو)**, with an explanation and examples. The eight bound classes on pp.388–389 are the source's own grouping; do not collapse them into the separate affix lists given by other grammarians on pp.374–385.
- **p.390:** Continues the bound-word section. The prose emphasizes that grammatical function and sentence context matter when identifying forms. It gives examples of bound constructions and distinguishes two explanatory subgroups in the author's account. Dense examples include repeated and segmented forms; they are not transcribed here as a normalized inventory.
- **p.391:** Continues the second subgroup and gives further examples of bound constructions, including forms analyzed into components. The page closes by contrasting forms that attach to or occur with other words and discussing their grammatical role. Preserve the printed examples as source data; do not silently resegment or standardize them.

## Cross-page comparison and limits

1. The grouping **کار (چاوگ)** on p.388 is a clear instance of the source grouping these together, in contrast with p.347 where **کار** and **چاوگ** appear as separate entries. This is a source-internal inconsistency/variation; retain both records rather than harmonizing them.
2. Pages 386–387 raise Prefix, Suffix, and Infix as questions/terms. The explicit question about whether Kurdish has an Infix must not be turned into an affirmative grammatical claim.
3. The independent/bound distinction on pp.388–391 is the book's own framework. It is not identical to the multiple attributed prefix/suffix accounts on pp.374–385.
4. The eight bound classes and the five independent classes are visible, but dense example lists on pp.390–391 need additional character-level checking before any lesson-ready transcription.
5. The citation to Evan C. Penglis remains separately attributed comparative material. No universal equivalence between the English grammatical categories and the book's Kurdish examples is inferred.
