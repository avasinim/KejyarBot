# Lesson 30 research note — Complex predicates and light verbs in Central Kurdish

Date: 2026-11-07  
Course: KejyarBot Course 4  
Lesson: 30 — کردارە لێکدراوەکان و کردارە سووکەکان

## Scope and terminology

This lesson covers complex predicates (also described in some Kurdish grammars as compound verbs), especially non-verbal element + light verb (NV + LV) constructions. It does not treat every multiword sequence as a compound predicate. It distinguishes:
- open-class combinations such as Noun + Verb and Adjective + Verb;
- adverbial, prepositional, prepositional-phrase, and preverbal-particle combinations;
- morphologically derived single-word verbs (Lesson 29);
- ordinary lexical/heavy verb uses, where the verb retains its full lexical meaning.

The terms “compound verb” and “complex predicate” are not used identically by all authors. The lesson names the relevant frameworks rather than pretending there is one uncontested taxonomy.

## Evidence and decisions

1. **Kareem (2016), Chapter 5** describes Central Kurdish complex predicates as an NV element followed by an inflected LV. The NV can be a noun, adjective, preverbal particle, preposition, adverb, or prepositional phrase. Examples include *ser birîn* (N+LV), *amade kirdin* (Adj+LV), *ser kewtin* (P+LV), *dreng kewtin* (Adv+LV), *ra-kirdin* (preverbal particle + LV), *le dest dan* and *le dayk bûn* (PP+LV).
2. **Light verbs** have reduced/bleached lexical content in the relevant construction and host verbal inflection (tense, mood, negation, agreement). The NV contributes much of the lexical meaning. A given verb may be a light verb in one context and a full lexical/heavy verb in another; “light” does not mean meaningless.
3. The argument structure of the whole predicate is jointly determined by the NV+LV combination. The same LV (e.g. *dan*) may form intransitive or transitive complex predicates depending on the NV. Therefore valency cannot be inferred from the LV alone.
4. Some nominal NV elements can be syntactically separated from the LV, and complex predicates are not necessarily single morphological words. This is important when teaching orthography: spacing, ZWNJ, joining, or hyphenation alone do not settle the morphological/syntactic analysis.
5. **Ahmadi (2021), §5.1.4** groups compound verbs into open-class compounds (especially N+V and Adj+V), particle verbs, and adpositional verbs; it notes *kirdin* and *bûn* as frequent in open-class compounds. This is a useful formal morphology framework, but the manuscript itself states in its abstract that it was still being completed.
6. **Kareem (2016)** analyzes preverbal particles as non-verbal components of complex predicates rather than ordinary verbal inflection. This differs in analytical framing from Ahmadi's grouping of particle verbs under compound verbs. Both are represented as distinct source-based analyses in the lesson.
7. **Ahmadi (2020), “A Tokenization System for the Kurdish Language,” §4.3/Table 2** documents orthographic variation in compound forms: they may be written together, separately, with ZWNJ, or with a hyphen. Consequently, orthographic spacing is not a sufficient diagnostic for compound status.

## Source-supported examples used

- *ser birîn* — head + cut, ‘behead’ (N + LV)
- *amade kirdin* — ready + do, ‘prepare’ (Adj + LV)
- *ser kewtin* — up + fall, ‘succeed’ (P + LV)
- *dreng kewtin* — late + fall, ‘be late’ (Adv + LV)
- *ra-kirdin* — preverbal particle + do, ‘run away’ (particle + LV)
- *le dest dan* — from hand + give, ‘lose’ (PP + LV)
- *le dayk bûn* — from mother + be, ‘be born’ (PP + LV)
- *bełên dan* — give a promise / promise (N + LV)
- *îstîqala dan* — resign (N + LV)

Sorani script forms in the lesson are normalized pedagogically, not claimed as the only possible local orthography: سەربڕین، ئامادەکردن، سەرکەوتن، درەنگ کەوتن، ڕاکردن، لە دەستدان، لە دایک بوون، بەڵێن دان، ئیستقالە دان.

## Why the proposed 19-pattern list is not copied as a fixed rule set

The owner-supplied list of 19 formation patterns is not treated as a closed, universal inventory. The academic sources support several broad structural types, but do not validate that exact list as a complete productive classification for all Sorani dialects. The lesson teaches the source-attested patterns and records the unverified status of the wider list rather than reproducing it as an established linguistic law.
