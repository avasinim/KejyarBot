## Findings and cautions

1. The source presents at least two distinct ways of classifying adverbs:
   - semantic/functional types, with the numbering extending to **11** across pp.246–248;
   - structural/formational types, including simple, non-simple, derived, and compound forms across pp.250–251.
   These schemes must remain separate unless the author explicitly connects them.
2. The six broad word classes on p.252 are a separate higher-level classification, not a summary of the adverb subtypes.
3. The examples on pp.241–245 show that a word's function in a sentence matters. Do not assign a permanent part-of-speech label based only on its surface form.
4. The comparative material on p.249 refers to Persian grammatical descriptions. Keep it attributed and separate from Kurdish-specific analysis.
5. A variety/dialect reference on p.243 needs a higher-resolution crop before exact naming. Do not turn the page's examples into a general rule for all Kurdish varieties.
6. This is a sequential page-level visual review. It is not a full transcription of all examples, lists, or attributed claims, and it does not constitute independent linguistic validation.
