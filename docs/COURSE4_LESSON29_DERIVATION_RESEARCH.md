# Lesson 29 research note — Simple and derived verbs in Sorani Kurdish

Date: 2026-11-07  
Course: KejyarBot Course 4  
Lesson: 29 — چاوگی سادە و داڕێژراو

## Scope

This lesson introduces simple and morphologically derived Sorani verb infinitives. It deliberately does **not** teach compound predicates in detail; noun/adjective + light-verb constructions, particle verbs as a broader class, and multiword complex predicates are expanded in Lesson 30. Nominal uses of infinitives are reserved for Lesson 31.

## Main analytical decisions

1. **Simple vs derived is a morphological distinction.** A simple verb has no derivational prefix/suffix in the relevant lexical analysis. A derived verb is built with a derivational morpheme and may create a new lexeme or a distinct lexical sense.
2. **Derivation vs inflection:** derivation creates a new lexeme or lexical meaning; inflection realizes grammatical forms of the same lexeme. The boundary can be language- and construction-specific; do not decide by spelling alone.
3. **Verbal particles/prefixes:** Ahmadi §5.1.4 distinguishes compound verbs, particle verbs, adpositional verbs, and morphologically-derived single-word verbs. The lesson uses established examples such as هەڵگرتن، داگرتن، ڕاگرتن، وەرگرتن but avoids presenting the full owner-supplied prefix list as a universally productive, closed inventory.
4. **Suffixal causatives:** Ahmadi lists مردن → مراندن and سووتان → سووتاندن, among other processes. In the infinitive analysis, distinguish the derivational past-stem material (e.g. -and) from the infinitive marker -in/-n. Therefore the teaching segmentation مراندن = مر + اند + ن and سووتاندن = سووت + اند + ن is a pedagogical segmentation, not a claim that the whole string -اندن is one indivisible morpheme in every analysis.
5. **-ەوە:** The suffix may be repetitive/inflectional in some environments and derivational/lexicalizing in others. Ahmadi gives contrasts such as کردنەوە vs کردن and خواردنەوە vs خواردن, while also describing a repetitive use. Do not classify every form in -ەوە as derived automatically.
6. **Orthography:** compound verbs may be written separately or connected depending on orthographic convention, while verbal particles may be written together as prefixes. Orthographic spacing is not, by itself, proof of morphological category.
7. **Dialect and evidence limits:** the paper explicitly limits its description to core Sorani characteristics and notes dialectal variation. The lesson's examples should not be generalized to every Sorani subdialect without further corpus/speaker validation.
