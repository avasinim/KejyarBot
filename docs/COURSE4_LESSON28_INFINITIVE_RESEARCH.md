# Course 4 — Lesson 28 research note

## Scope

Lesson 28 introduces the Sorani Kurdish infinitive (چاوگ), distinguishes the citation form from finite person-marked verb forms, and explains the five traditional groups of past stems. It does not yet teach the morphology of simple/derived/compound verbs or the syntactic nominal use of infinitives; those topics are reserved for Lessons 29–31.

## Main correction to the owner-supplied notes

The labels «یایی، واوی، ئەلفی، دالی، تایی» are retained as familiar teaching labels, but their analysis is corrected: they refer to five groups of **past stems**, classified by the final segment remaining after the infinitive marker is removed. They are not five independent types of infinitive.

Kareem (2016: 15–16) explicitly lists the five past-stem endings as -î, -û, -a, -d, and -t:

| Past-stem ending | Examples in Sorani orthography | Stem illustration |
|---|---|---|
| -î | فڕین، کڕین | فڕیـ، کڕیـ |
| -û | بوون، دروون | بووـ، درووـ |
| -a | هێنان، کێشان | هێناـ، کێشاـ |
| -d | ناردن، خوێندن، مردن، بردن | ناردـ، خوێندـ، مردـ، بردـ |
| -t | هاتن، کەوتن، گرتن | هاتـ، کەوتـ، گرتـ |

The exact segment is identified from the **past stem**, not by mechanically reading only the letter immediately before the final «ن» in the infinitive. This avoids conflating stem-final material with the infinitive marker.

## Infinitive and the two stems

Haig (2018: 276–277, §3.2.2) describes the infinitive as the traditional citation form for Kurdish verbs, formed from the past stem with -(i)n, and gives a Sorani table contrasting infinitive, past stem and present stem. The chapter identifies its Sorani description as based primarily on the Suleymaniyya variety and cautions that spoken varieties and dialects differ.

Kareem (2016: 15–16) explains that deleting the infinitive marker -(î)n yields the past stem. He cautions that the label “past stem” does not mean that the stem itself contains tense; it is the stem used in past-tense constructions. The derivation of the present stem from the past stem is not always predictable and may involve deletion or phonological change. The lesson therefore does not teach a single exceptionless conversion rule.

Ahmadi (2021), in the manuscript *A Formal Description of Sorani Kurdish Morphology* (arXiv:2109.03942), likewise describes -in as the infinitive suffix and relates infinitive, past stem and present stem. The manuscript itself states that it was being completed; it is therefore treated as a useful formal reference, not as a sole or final authority.

## Finite form versus infinitive

The owner-supplied example «خواردم» is useful for contrast, but it is not an infinitive. In the lesson it is described at a teaching level as a past-stem form «خواردـ» with a person/agreement ending «ـم». The example is used only to distinguish a finite form from the dictionary/citation form «خواردن»; it is not intended as a complete account of Sorani past transitive agreement, which is more complex.

## Gorani/Hawrami comparison

The supplied forms «واردەیێ، مەنەیێ، خوایێ، ڕەماییێ» are not used to formulate Sorani rules. Haig (2018) treats Sorani and Gorani as distinct groups in the Iranian languages of northern Iraq and notes systematic morphological differences. Because the supplied forms have not been independently verified here with a dialect-specific reference and speaker data, the lesson preserves them only as an owner-supplied comparative note requiring separate verification.

## Limits and excluded material

- The five-group analysis is a descriptive classification of past stems in a particular Central Kurdish grammatical tradition; it is not presented as the only possible theoretical analysis of every Sorani variety.
- The exact pronunciation and spelling of forms can vary by variety; the lesson's Sorani spellings are pedagogical renderings.
- No representative spoken corpus was used to validate every supplied form or every dialectal variant.
- `Rezmany_axawtny_kurdy.pdf` is excluded by owner instruction and was not consulted, mapped, or used in any way.
