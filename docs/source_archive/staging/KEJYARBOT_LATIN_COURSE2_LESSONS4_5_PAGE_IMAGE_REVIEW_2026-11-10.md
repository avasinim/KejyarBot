## Lesson 4 — Number (§§47–51, pp.55–56)
- §47 explains singular/plural distinction as a grammatical category; the draft's general definition is aligned.
- §§48–49 give noun examples such as `Mîr hat / Mîr hatin`, `Ez hespêkî dibînim` and plural `Ez hespina dibînim`-type contexts; the draft's examples and caution that number must be distinguished from case are consistent with the source.
- §50 explicitly notes that singular/plural distinctions can surface through several interacting mechanisms, including verbal form, case, and determiners. The draft teaches number separately first, then links it to inflection in Lesson 5; this is an appropriate pedagogical separation.
- §51 notes that proper names do not ordinarily pluralize in the same way as common nouns, except in figurative or illustrative uses. The reviewed draft does not make an overbroad claim about pluralizing proper names.

## Lesson 5 — Case/inflection (§§52–58, pp.57–60)
- §§52–54 explain that Kurdish is an inflectional language, that only certain parts of speech inflect, and that nouns generally remain uninflected as subjects while objects may be inflected. The reviewed draft's basic distinction is aligned.
- §55 gives the familiar noun patterns: masculine singular `-î`, feminine singular `-ê`, plural `-an`, with examples such as `Ez hespî dibînim`, `Ez mehînê dibînim`, `Ez hespan dibînim`, `Ez mehînan dibînim`.
- §56 is an important qualification: in transitive past constructions, the agent/subject is inflected while the direct object remains uninflected. The earlier draft's broad phrase that objects are inflected could be misread as universal. A source-specific caution and representative examples have now been added to the reviewed draft.
- §57 gives three accepted variants for certain masculine nouns with final `e/a` vowel changes under inflection, and lists lexical exceptions that do not take the expected change. The reviewed draft preserves the principal accepted variants and warns against applying a single rule to all nouns.
- §58 discusses inflection in groups of multiple nouns: the final noun's gender/number can determine how the group behaves, and not every noun in the group is necessarily inflected. This advanced qualification has now been added to the reviewed draft.
