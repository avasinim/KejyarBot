## Findings
- §65 (p.63–64) defines indefinite determiners and distinguishes two structural environments according to whether a following nominal complement follows the noun.
- §66 (p.64) gives `-ek` for masculine singular, `-ek` for feminine singular, and `-in` for plural when no following complement is present. Examples include `Hespek hat`, `Mehînek hat`, `Hespin hatin`, `Mehînin hatin`.
- §67 (p.65) gives `-ekî/-eke/-ine` when a following complement/modifier is present: `Hespekî delal hat`, `Mehîneke delal hat`, `Mehînine delal hatin`. The lesson should not blur this pattern with the next pattern.
- §68 (p.66) separately gives forms placed before adjectives: `-î/-e/-en`, e.g. `Hespekî min î qenc hebû`, `Mehîneke min e qenc hebû`, `Hespine min en qenc hebûn`. The earlier lesson draft omitted this complete pattern; it has now been added.
- §69 (pp.66–67) explains that when multiple complements follow a noun, the determiner's form is governed by the gender/number of the final complement. The reviewed draft now records this as an advanced scope note.
- §70 (p.67) gives oblique forms `-ekî/-ekê/-ina` when the noun is not the subject but is in object position. Examples include `Ez hespekî dibînim`, `Ez mehînekê dibînim`, and `Ez hespina dibînim`. The reviewed draft now explicitly distinguishes these from the basic forms.
- §71 (p.68) discusses compounds with a determined noun and indefinite modifier; this is a related extension and not a new base suffix paradigm.
