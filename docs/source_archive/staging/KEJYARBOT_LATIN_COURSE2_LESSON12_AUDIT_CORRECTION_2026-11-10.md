## Why this correction is necessary
A fresh check of the source PDF's printed table of contents and the actual body pages found errors in the earlier Lesson 12 comparison note. The earlier note incorrectly placed the adjective section on pp.86–88 and described several source labels as editorial alternatives. Those claims are withdrawn.

The source body confirms:
- §128 begins the degrees of descriptive adjectives, printed p.91.
- §131 begins demonstrative adjectives, printed p.93.
- §134 divides numeral adjectives into `jmarîn` and `rêzîn`, printed p.94.
- §§137–139 cover interrogative, indefinite, and possessive adjectives, printed pp.95–96.
- §§141–143 cover numerals, definite/indefinite numerals, and the four subtypes of definite numerals, printed pp.97–98.
- §§148–152 discuss `nîv`, `yek`, and `hezar`, printed pp.98–100.

## Corrected terminology findings
1. `rengdêrên wesfîn` is the source's own heading (p.90), not an editorial replacement for `çilobûn û çawabûnê`.
2. `rengdêrên xwemalîn` is the source's own heading (p.95), not an editorial replacement for `destdaniyê/xwedîtiyê`.
3. `rengdêrên jmarîn` and `rengdêrên rêzîn` are the source's own categories in §134 (p.94). The earlier note's suggestion that `rêzîn` should be mapped to a source label `rêzda` is withdrawn.
4. The source's definite numeral subtypes are `bingehîn`, `rêzîn`, `birewîn`, and `komelîn` (§143, p.97). The draft's description of this four-part division is supported.
5. The distinction between numeral adjective and numeral pronoun remains a useful teaching distinction, but the explanation should be grounded in syntax and the source's definition of `jmarnav`, not in the fact that the book has separate chapter headings.

## Additional confirmation from source examples
- Demonstrative adjective paradigms include `vî hespî / van hespan`, `wî hespî / wan hespan`, `vê mehinê / van mehinan`, and `wê mehinê / wan mehinan` (§132, p.93).
- Numeral adjectives are divided into cardinal and ordinal; the source places cardinal numeral adjectives before nouns and ordinal numeral adjectives after nouns (§§134–136, pp.94–95).
- Indefinite adjectives listed in §138 include `tu, her, herçî, filan, gelek, hemî, hevqas, çend` (p.95).
- Possessive adjective examples in §139 include `hespê min`, `mehînên te`, `kitêba vî`, `qelemên wê`, `xaniyê me`, `çiyayê we`, `kurên van`, and `keçên wan` (pp.95–96).
- §148 says standalone `nîv` takes `-î` (e.g. `nîvî)); when combined with another numeral or noun it remains `nîv` and the associated noun follows the relevant inflection.
- §149 says `yek` takes `-ê` in the specified standalone/compound numeral forms, but before a noun it remains `yek`; the noun then inflects.
- §152 says standalone `hezar` takes `-î` and may cause inflection of its associated noun; when preceded by another numeral it follows the final numeral's inflection pattern.
