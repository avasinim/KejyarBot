## Methodological distinction
The source uses “ناوبەند/ناوەندگر” for several phenomena that must not be collapsed into one category:

1. **Morphological linker/interfix in word formation:** a bound element inside a lexical compound, with evidence for its function, distribution, and word-level status.
2. **Free grammatical word or particle inside a phrase:** e.g. a preposition or conjunction that may be written adjacent to neighboring words in an example.
3. **Reduplicative/paired construction:** two related bases form a semantic pair; the intervening sound or sequence may be part of a constructional pattern, not necessarily a productive interfix.
4. **Phonological linking/hiatus repair:** a consonant may surface between vowels or morphemes without being a word-forming interfix.
5. **Lexicalized expression:** a historically or synchronically fixed word whose internal segmentation is not automatically productive.

For every candidate, ask: Is the element bound? Does it have a stable grammatical/semantic contribution? Does it recur with more than one base in a predictable pattern? Is the result a lexical/phonological word rather than a phrase? Are stress, dialect, speaker judgments, and dictionary/corpus attestations consistent?

Assessment:
- This is a larger and more coherent candidate set than the earlier excerpt showed. Several forms exhibit paired, directional, spatial, or distributive/reduplicative semantics.
- Do not automatically analyze every written `ەو` as one invariant morpheme. Some examples may contain `ە + و`, a sequence associated with the first base, a coordinating element, or a lexicalized construction. Forms such as `بەرەوپاش`, `بەرەوپێڵ`, `سەرەوژوور`, `سەرەوخوار`, and `ڕاستەوخۆ` need separate morphological and lexical analysis rather than being grouped solely by spelling.
- Provisional classification: **paired/reduplicative and directional compound candidates**. The source supports recording the pattern, but not yet a universal productive `-ەو-` interfix rule.
- Required next evidence: dictionary/corpus attestations for each item, dialect and meaning notes, stress/phonological-word tests, and comparison with free coordination or adpositional phrases.

### C. `-ان-`
Owner-supplied formations:
- `بەر + ان + بەر = بەرانبەر`
- `دەور + ان + دەور = دەوراندەور`
- `سەر + ان + سەر = سەرانسەر`
- Further list: `دەساندەس، دۆراندۆر، شەقانشەق، لێوانلێو`.

Assessment:
- These forms may reflect lexicalized paired constructions, sound/shape alternation, or historical morphology. The sequence `ان` is also familiar as a plural marker in Kurdish, so a simple “middle position = interfix” analysis is insufficient.
- Provisional classification: **lexicalized/reduplicative candidates; not a confirmed productive `-ان-` interfix class**.
- Analyze each item's meaning, base recoverability, stress, and whether `ان` contributes a stable linker function rather than plural or lexical material.

## Separate phonological linking-consonant subsection
The source then shifts from word-compounding elements to “ناوبەندی دەنگی” and introduces:
- `ج`: proposed in `میوەجات، سەوزیجات، سەوزەجات`, described as appearing between a vowel-final base and the plural ending `-ات`.
- `ه`: proposed in `دێهات، میوەهات` with `-ات`, and in ordinal forms `نۆهەم، دووهەم، سێهەم`; it also gives `دێهاتەکان`.
- `د`: the supplied excerpt ends immediately after the start of its definition, so no classification is possible yet.

These must not be mixed with morphological interfixes in lexical compounds. In particular:
- The `ج` in forms ending in `-ات` is presented by the source as a phonological bridge/hiatus-related consonant, not as a compound linker. Its distribution and lexical status still need independent checking.
- The `ه` examples span more than one environment (plural formation and ordinal formation); they should be separated and tested rather than treated as one universal epenthetic rule.
- The `د` section remains incomplete in the preserved source excerpt. Do not invent its examples or rule.

## Recommended safe teaching structure
1. Retain the research-supported core sections for `-ە-` and `-و-`, with exact citations and examples from Huseen & Shuani (2024).
2. Add a separate “candidate linker patterns” appendix for `ەو، او، ان، بە` and the earlier `لە، نە، تا، نا` lists. Preserve all original examples but label their status individually.
3. Keep `ج، ه، د` under a separate heading: “phonological linking consonants / hiatus-related alternations — pending verification”, not under word-compounding interfixes.
4. Create a contrast exercise that asks learners to distinguish:
   - a bound linker in a compound;
   - the free conjunction `و` or preposition `بە/لە`;
   - a reduplicative or paired lexical form;
   - a phonological bridge between vowels;
   - a fixed lexicalized expression.
5. Do not change Lesson 3 runtime until the full source is reconciled, each example is checked, and the academic teaching layout is approved through the normal review process.
