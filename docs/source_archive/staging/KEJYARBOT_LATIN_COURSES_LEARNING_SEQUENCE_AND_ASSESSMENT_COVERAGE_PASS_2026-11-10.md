## Scope

## Course 1 — Latin alphabet and orthography
The sequence moves from the 31-letter inventory and letter forms (Lessons 1–2), through vowel/consonant classification and vowel groups (Lessons 3–7), to V/W and X/Y/Z contrasts (Lessons 8–9), the distinction between written letters and spoken sounds (Lesson 10), source-table frequency interpretation (Lesson 11), and cumulative review (Lesson 12).

Coverage observations:
- Lesson 1 states its learning outcome inline; it is not missing an objective.
- Lessons 2–12 each state learning outcomes, including Lessons 6 and 7 where the objective heading uses a different Markdown style.
- Lesson 10's sound-versus-letter explanation follows the prior alphabet and vowel/consonant work, and Lesson 1 explicitly previews that distinction. The sequence is coherent.
- Lesson 11 follows the alphabet inventory and introduces source-table counts as a separate interpretive topic; it does not present the source counts as universal phoneme-frequency claims.
- Lesson 9's Y question is explicitly scoped to the source's alphabetic classification, avoiding an unsupported generalization about every phonetic realization.

## Course 2 — Kurdish grammar foundations
The sequence moves from grammar and language units (Lesson 1), to nouns and grammatical gender (Lessons 2–3), number and inflection (Lessons 4–5), noun modifiers and definiteness (Lessons 6–8), personal/agent and object/reflexive pronouns (Lessons 9–10), demonstrative/relative/interrogative and related pronouns (Lesson 11), and adjective/numeral classes (Lesson 12).

Coverage observations:
- The progression from noun categories to gender, number, inflection, and noun modifiers is pedagogically intelligible.
- Lessons 9–11 build from personal and agent forms to object/reflexive forms and then demonstrative/relative/interrogative forms. The reviewed drafts distinguish grammatical roles rather than treating all pronoun forms as interchangeable.
- Lesson 12 consolidates adjective and numeral classes after noun structure, inflection, and pronoun work. It includes a separate distinction between numeral adjectives and the independently classified numeral category (`jmarnav`).
- The current drafts contain practice and quiz material within each lesson; no clear case was found in this pass where a later lesson's quiz requires a concept that is wholly absent from all preceding or current lesson content.
