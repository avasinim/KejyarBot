## Lesson 6 — Noun modifiers (§§59–60, p.61)
- §59 defines the noun modifier as a word or group of words that accompanies a noun and narrows or adds information to its meaning.
- The source explicitly gives pre-nominal examples: `ev keçik`, `kîjan mirov`, `çar xanî`, `hin çiya`, `çend kitêb`, `deh bizin`, `van zarokan`.
- It also gives post-nominal examples: `hespê beza`, `mehîna spehî`, `çiyayê bilind`, `xencera min`. The reviewed draft's selected examples are faithful.
- §60 shows that a noun can take more than one modifier, including nested genitive-like groups such as `xencera xulamê min`, `xencera xulamê pismamê min`, and `xencera xulamê çîranê pismamê min`. The draft includes a representative nested example and explains the multi-element structure.
- Terminological warning is preserved: `bireserê navdêrê` in this lesson means a noun modifier, not the direct object of a clause.

## Lesson 7 — Definite determiners (§§61–64, pp.62–63)
- §61 defines the determiner as a small word placed before or after a noun to identify it or limit its reference. The source says determiners show gender and number and can make a noun definite or indefinite.
- §62 distinguishes definite and indefinite determiners.
- §63 gives the definite forms `ê` for masculine singular, `a` for feminine singular, and `ên` for plural across genders. The reviewed draft's paradigm and examples align.
- §64 explains that definite determiners can occur before adjectives when the noun is understood/omitted, e.g. `Ez ê gewir dixwazim`, `Ez a sor dixwazim`, `Ez ên sor dixwazim`. The reviewed draft includes this distinct construction and correctly warns not to confuse these independent forms with suffixes attached after nouns.
