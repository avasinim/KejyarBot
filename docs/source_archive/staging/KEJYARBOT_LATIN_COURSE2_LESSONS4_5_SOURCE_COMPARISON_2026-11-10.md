## Lesson 4 — Mêjer
### Source-supported
- The draft's singular/plural contrast corresponds to §48–50, printed pp.55–56.
- `Mîr hat.` / `Mîr hatin.` occurs in §48 (p.55). In this grammatical context `mîr` is treated as a countable common noun/title, not as a person's proper name. The draft's clarification is useful and prevents a misleading reading of capitalization at sentence start.
- `Hesp hat / Hesp hatin` and `Mehîn hat / Mehîn hatin` are source examples in §55 (p.57), not §48; the draft uses them as additional illustration of number and later inflection.
- `Ez hespî dibînim / Ez hespan dibînim` and `Ez mehînê dibînim / Ez mehînan dibînim` are also in §55 (p.57).
- The `mehînek / mehînin` and `mehîneke / mehînine` patterns occur in the later indefinite-suffix discussion (§§66–67, pp.64–66). They are not the core source examples for the number section on pp.55–56. If retained in Lesson 4 as a preview, keep the editorial signpost and avoid implying they are the general plural paradigm.

### Source nuance preserved
§§48–50 explicitly warn that Kurmanji number forms may be affected by case, inflection, and suffixes; number is not always visible as a simple isolated ending. The draft's warning that `hespan` and `mehînan` are inflected forms rather than a universal plural marker is consistent with that nuance.

## Lesson 5 — Tewanga Navdêran
### Source-supported
- The definition of inflection as changes in a word's form according to its role/location in the sentence corresponds to §§52–54 (p.57).
- The general pattern — masculine singular with `-î`, feminine singular with `-ê`, plural with `-an` — is stated in §55 (p.57).
- The distinction between subject forms and object forms is supported by §55 (p.57), while the transitive past subject/agent examples are further explained in §56 (p.58).
- The examples `Ez hespî dibînim / Ez hespan dibînim` and `Ez mehînê dibînim / Ez mehînan dibînim` are source examples (§55, p.57).
- The three variants `Soro kevirî tavêje`, `Soro kêvir tavêje`, and `Soro kevir tavêje` are source examples in the note under §57 (p.59). The source explicitly presents all three as acceptable variants in its discussion.
- The draft's uninflected masculine noun examples `kum, goşt, dil, hiş, mirov, mêr, gund` are a subset of the source list in §57 (p.59). The source list also includes `mêrik, gir, kur, mîr`; the draft need not reproduce every item if it is presented as a selected list.
- The draft's `maseyan → masan` and `çiyayan → çiyan` examples correspond to the source's paired examples on p.60 (§58), where plural inflection removes the intervening `e/a` and uses the relevant `-an` form.

### Editorial/teaching additions
- The draft's compact summary that inflection differs from number is a teaching synthesis across §§52–58, not a verbatim source sentence.
- The draft's comparison of `hespan/mehînan` with `hespin/mehînin` is a useful cross-reference: the former are inflected plural forms in §55, while the latter belong to the indefinite plural suffix system in §§66–67 (pp.64–66). Keep the distinction explicit.
- The list of uninflected masculine nouns is selected, not exhaustive; label it as examples rather than the complete source list.
