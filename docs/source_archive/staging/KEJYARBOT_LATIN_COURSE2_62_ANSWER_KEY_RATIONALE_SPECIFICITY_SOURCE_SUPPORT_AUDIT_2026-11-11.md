## 2. Method and rating criteria

Each rationale was considered against four questions:
1. **Specificity:** Does it explain why this answer fits this exact item, rather than simply announcing that it is correct?
2. **Rule/example:** Does it state the relevant lesson rule or analyze the tested example?
3. **Contrast:** Where a likely confusion exists, does it distinguish the answer from the closest distractor or a neighboring grammatical function?
4. **Source/scope:** Does it cite the relevant section where useful, and does it avoid presenting a lesson-specific convention as a universal claim?

Ratings:
- **A — explanatory:** item-specific reason, relevant distinction and/or source anchor are present.
- **B — useful but incomplete:** a correct rule is stated, but contrast, context, or source grounding could be improved.
- **C — generic/restatement:** answer is confirmed but the rationale does not explain it.
- **H — hold:** an unresolved terminology, scope, or answer-to-stem issue means the rationale must not be used to certify the item.

## 5. Recommended rationale standard for a future approved revision

Use a concise three-part structure, not a fixed template sentence:
1. **Answer-specific rule:** name the relevant grammatical category/form/function.
2. **Evidence:** point to the exact example or section in the lesson/source where helpful.
3. **Contrast/scope:** state the closest distinction that prevents a likely misunderstanding.

Examples of the *shape* (not approved replacement text):
- “`tîp` is the written sign representing a speech sound; the sound itself is not the letter.”
- “In the present-tense example `Ez mehînê dibînim`, `mehînê` is the feminine singular object form in §55; do not transfer the transitive-past agent pattern in §56.”
- “In `Wê ew dît`, the lesson labels `wê` as the inflected agent and `ew` as the direct object; this example therefore cannot by itself support a stem that calls `wê` the object form.”

These examples illustrate specificity only. They do not approve an answer-key change or settle open terminology.
