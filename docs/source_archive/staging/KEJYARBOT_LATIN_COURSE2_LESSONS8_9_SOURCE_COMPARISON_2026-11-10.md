## Lesson 8 — Veqetandekên Nebinavkirî

### Source-supported
- The source defines indefinite markers as forms attached after nouns to indicate an unidentified/unspecified person or thing (§65, p.63).
- The three bare-noun forms in §66 (p.64) are `-ek` for masculine singular, `-ek` for feminine singular, and `-in` for plural. The source examples include `hespek hat`, `hespin hatin`, `mehînek hat`, and `mehînin hatin`.
- Where a modifier follows the noun, §67 (p.65) gives `-ekî`, `-eke`, and `-ine`; examples include `hespekî delal hat`, `hespine delal hatin`, `mehîneke delal hat`, and `mehînîne delal hatin`.
- §68 (p.66) gives the pre-adjective forms `-î`, `-e`, and `-en`, with examples such as `hespekî min î qenc`, `mehîneke min e qenc`, and their plural counterparts.
- §70 (p.67) explains that indefinite-marked nouns in non-agent/direct-object position are declined; examples include `Min hespek dît`, `Min hespin dîtin`, `Min mehînek dît`, and `Min mehînin dîtin`. The source also notes regional variation in the forms used for plural `-in` in oblique contexts.

### Editorial clarifications and issue to track
1. The draft's distinction between bare-noun `-ek/-ek/-in` and modifier-followed `-ekî/-eke/-ine` is supported by §§66–67.
2. The draft's note that oblique forms vary with grammatical role is supported by §§69–70 and the regional note on p.68. Keep the variants clearly labelled; do not present one regional variant as universal.
3. The plural modifier-followed example in the draft is written `mehînine delal hatin`. The source's printed example is segmented/OCR-rendered as `Mehînîne delal hatin`; spelling should be checked against the page image and the project's orthographic policy before any normalization. Do not silently alter it in this audit.
4. The draft's phrase “bireser/rengdêr li pey were” is a learner-facing shorthand. The source's criterion is a noun followed by a modifier; the course should not imply that only direct objects or only adjectives can trigger this pattern.

## Lesson 9 — Pronavên Kesîn û Kirar

### Source-supported
- The source distinguishes three pronoun genders: masculine, feminine, and neutral (§73, p.71). `wî` is masculine, `wê` feminine, and the remaining personal-pronoun forms are grouped as neutral (§75, p.72).
- The source lists personal pronouns by person: first person `ez, min, em, me, xwe`; second person `tu, te, hon, we, xwe`; third person `ew, wî, wê, ew, wan, xwe, ê` (§75, p.72).
- The source explicitly distinguishes two personal-pronoun sets: agent forms and direct-object forms (§77, pp.72–73). The table in the draft correctly reproduces the core pairs: `ez/min`, `tu/te`, `ew/wî,wê`, `em/me`, `hon/we`, `ew/wan`.
- The role alternation in past transitive clauses is directly supported by §78 (p.73): in these constructions the agent and direct-object pronouns exchange roles. Source examples include `Min got`, `Te xwar`, `Wî xist`, `Wê xwar`, `We got`, `Wan xist`, and `Me xwar`.
- The note that a pronoun form must not be labelled agent in every sentence is important and consistent with the source's explanation of the two sets and their syntactic functions.

### Omission / precision issue
1. The draft's personal-pronoun table omits `xwe` and `ê`. The source explicitly includes both in the personal-pronoun inventory (§75, p.72), while also treating `xwe` in its dedicated later section. This is a scope/coverage omission, not a contradiction in the six core agent/object pairs. Add a cross-reference or an explicit note that the table is limited to the basic agent/direct-object paradigm; do not silently imply the table is the complete personal-pronoun inventory.
2. The source's third-person neutral inventory includes `ew`, `xwe`, and `ê`; `wî` and `wê` are the gender-marked forms. The draft's neutral row for `ew` is therefore compatible, but the dash in its agent-form column should be described as “not separately tabulated here,” not as proof that the source has no relevant form.
3. `hon` is retained as the course's chosen second-person plural form. This is a course editorial selection consistent with the source's list, not an error.
4. The draft's separate statement that `min/te/wî/wê/me/we/wan` are “forma kirarî” is acceptable only as the name of the agent-form paradigm. It must not be read as saying these forms always function as agents; the draft's own caveat correctly prevents that misreading.
