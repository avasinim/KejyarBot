## Audit method and limits
Every stem, option set, keyed answer and available rationale was inventoried. Each item was considered for:
1. target overlap/redundancy within and across lessons;
2. distractor plausibility, overlap and parallelism;
3. defensibility of exactly one keyed answer in the taught context;
4. sequencing and whether later-course knowledge is assumed;
5. consistency with the taught terminology and lesson examples.

This is an editorial/source-alignment review, not learner-response analysis, psychometric validation, native-speaker certification, runtime testing or proof that every possible dialectal interpretation has been eliminated.

## Cross-item and course-level observations

### A. Most valuable redundancy reductions
1. **Lesson 2:** Q1/Q5 both assess the general/common-noun concept; Q2–Q4 already provide classification examples. Recast Q5 as a context contrast or use it for a less-covered distinction.
2. **Lesson 3:** Q2 and Q3 both ask for masculine nouns from the same list. Replace one with a feminine/context-sensitive example.
3. **Lesson 4:** Q2 and Q5 both test `hespin` as plural; Q3 also tests the singular counterpart. Retain one direct pair and use the freed item for a distinct application.
4. **Lesson 12:** Q1 and Q2/Q3 all test adjective identification at different levels. This is not wholly redundant, but Q1 should use more discriminating alternatives and Q4/Q5 should secure the taxonomy/numeral targets.

### B. Distractor quality
Most clearly weak distractor sets occur in L1 Q1, L2 Q1, L5 Q1/Q5, L7 Q5 and L12 Q1. In these, one or both incorrect options are obviously unrelated to the stem's grammatical category. Improve plausibility without introducing alternative defensible answers. L5 Q1 should use the previously recorded refined contrast; L5 Q5 should use bounded, teachable overgeneralizations rather than absurd alternatives.

### C. Single-answer defensibility and ambiguity controls
- L8 Q2–Q7 depend on construction conditions. Preserve the explicit following-modifier/complement and object-case conditions; avoid compressing these into context-free suffix questions.
- L10 Q1 is the clearest stem-precision issue: `min` belongs to the course's object-pronoun inventory but is also the agent form in Lesson 9's past transitive table. Ask inventory membership or show an unambiguous object slot.
- L11 Q3/Q4 intentionally distinguish `ko` as relative pronoun from `ko` as clause linker. Keep their examples and terminology distinct.
- L12 Q4 is taxonomy-specific; retain “according to this course's classification”.
- L12 Q5 needs a source-coverage check for the example `nîv` before considering it fully instructionally grounded.

### D. Sequencing
- Do not introduce the `hespan/hespin` inflection contrast as a Lesson 4 quiz item; it presupposes Lesson 5's tewang content. It remains suitable for a cumulative exercise after Lesson 5.
- Lesson 8's object-case forms are late-lesson content and should be placed after their explanation.
- The Lesson 9 four-cell paradigm repetition is pedagogically defensible because each item checks a distinct person/number/gender cell.
