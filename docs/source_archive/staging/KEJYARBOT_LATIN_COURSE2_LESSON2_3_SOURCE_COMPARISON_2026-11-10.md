## Lesson 2 — Navdêr: Hevenav û Serenav
### Source-supported
- The draft's basic noun definition corresponds to §34: a noun indicates a person, animal, or thing.
- The common-noun definition corresponds to §36: a common noun refers to more than one person, animal, or thing as a class.
- The proper-noun definition corresponds to §37: a proper noun identifies one particular person, animal, or thing.
- The draft's source-name list is consistent with §37: Sado, Bêlan, Herekol, Hevind, Sîpan, Xabûr, Hindistan, Hezro, Kerevan, Zîn, Dêrgul, Kurdistan, Agirî, Cemşîd, Ferezende.
- The additional names Ferzane, Kejyar, and Argesh are explicitly labelled supplemental editorial examples in the draft, not source examples.

### Editorial distinction
The draft's explanation that a common noun names a class and a proper noun identifies an individual is a teaching paraphrase of §§36–37, not a verbatim quotation.

## Lesson 3 — Zayenda Navdêran
### Source-supported
- §39 states that Kurmanji has two grammatical genders: feminine and masculine.
- §40's masculine list includes: Cemşîd, mêş, nêrî, ga, xanî, gir, Ferat, can, beran, Xurşîd, derî, êzing, hiş, çem, kevir, law, dirhav.
- §41's feminine list includes: Sînem xan, jin, meh, dar, çêlek, mehîn, tifing, bizin, bîr, gol, pehnav, Herekol, Hezarom, co, enî, top, ejnû, Kurdistan.
- The draft correctly preserves the source's classification of `Herekol` as feminine.
- The draft's explanation of `dar` having different gender according to meaning is consistent with the source's grammatical treatment elsewhere in this section and its examples; retain the precise contrast and page/paragraph locator in the lesson-level provenance when fully mapped.

### Discrepancies / review flags
1. **Source-list omissions:** the current Course 2 Lesson 3 feminine list omits two items present in §41: `Sînem xan` and `Hezarom`. This is an omission relative to the source list, not proof that the lesson is wrong; decide whether the course intends a complete source inventory or a selected teaching list. Do not silently insert them.
2. **Supplemental names:** `Argesh`, `Ferzane`, and `Kejyar` are not in the corresponding source lists on p.52. The draft already labels them as added practice names; keep that provenance label adjacent to the list so they cannot be mistaken for Celadet's examples.
3. **Spelling consistency:** the draft uses `mehin` in the feminine list, while the source's form is `mehîn` and the course uses `mehîn` elsewhere. Flag for a controlled spelling correction after the source comparison, not a global replacement.
4. The draft's statement that grammatical gender need not match biological sex is an editorial teaching explanation. The source gives grammatical classes and examples; avoid attributing this explanatory formulation as a direct quotation.
