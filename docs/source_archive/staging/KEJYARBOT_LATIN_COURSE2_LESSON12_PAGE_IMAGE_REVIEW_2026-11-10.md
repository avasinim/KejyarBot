# Course 2 Lesson 12 — Source page-image verification
**Date:** 2026-11-10  
**Branch:** `archive/preservation-audit-2026-11-10`  
**Source:** Celadet Alî Bedir-Xan, *Elfabêya kurdî & Bingehên gramera kurdmancî*, Nefel, Stockholm, 2002, printed pp.89–100, §§119–152.

## Page-image checks
The scanned page images, not OCR alone, were inspected for printed pp.89–100.

- **§§119–123, pp.89–90:** The source defines adjectives by their relationship to nouns and divides them into exactly six classes: `wesfîn`, `îşarkî`, `jmarîn`, `pirsiyarkî`, `nebinavkirî`, and `xwemalîn`. It states the usual positions: descriptive adjectives after nouns; possessive adjectives after nouns with a linking marker; demonstrative, interrogative, and indefinite adjectives before nouns; numeral adjectives may occur before or after nouns. The draft's six-class inventory is aligned.
- **§§124–127, pp.90–91:** Descriptive adjectives may be used as predicates and can occur without an expressed noun. When an adjective is used substantively, noun-like inflection/determiner behaviour can apply. The draft's examples are teaching selections; retain the distinction between source rule and simplified classroom explanation.
- **§§128–130, pp.91–92:** The source explicitly illustrates comparative formation with `-tir`, e.g. `genç/gençtir`, `bilind/bilindtir`, `spehî/spehîtir`, `sor/sortir`, and `bez/bezatir`. The draft's `mezintir` is consistent with this pattern. The form `herî mezin` is not shown in this source section; it may be retained only as a clearly labelled modern/editorial teaching supplement, not as a direct Celadet example. The reviewed draft has been clarified accordingly.
- **§§131–133, pp.93–94:** Demonstrative adjectives precede nouns; forms change with gender, number, and oblique case. The source gives `vî hespî / van hespan`, `wî hespî / wan hespan`, `vê mehînê / van mehînan`, and `wê mehînê / wan mehînan`. In noun groups with multiple nouns, the demonstrative can mark the last noun; number/gender agreement conditions are explained. The reviewed draft's forms are consistent.
- **§§134–136, pp.94–95:** Numeral adjectives have two kinds: `jmarîn` and `rêzîn`. The source gives cardinal examples including `Deh mirov hatin / Deh mirovan got` and `Çel mirov hatin / Çel mirovan got`; ordinal adjectives are placed after nouns in examples such as `stûna çaran`, `a pêncan`, and `ê heftan`. The draft's distinction and examples are aligned.
- **§§137–140, pp.95–96:** Interrogative adjective examples include `kîjan mirov`, `çend mirovan`, and `çend mirov`; indefinite adjective examples include `tu`, `her`, `herçî`, `filan`, `gelek`, `hemî`, `hevqas`, and `çend`; possessive adjectives are formed with personal and demonstrative pronouns and follow the noun, with the linking marker selected according to noun gender/number. Draft's sample inventory is a subset, not a full list.
- **§§141–145, pp.97–98:** Numerals identify number/quantity and can occupy noun-like positions; the source distinguishes definite and indefinite numerals. Definite numerals are divided into four classes: basic, ordinal, fractional, and collective. The draft's labels `bingehîn`, `rêzîn`, `birewîn`, `komelîn` track the source's four categories. Indefinite examples include `gelek`, `hindik`, `pir`, `bêtir`, and `zehf`.
- **§§146–148, p.98:** When a numeral is used as a direct object, it may be inflected; the noun following it may also be inflected. The source explicitly says that inflection in the oblique case primarily affects simple numerals, and that for compound numerals the final number receives inflection. The special `nîv` forms include `nîvî` and `nîvekî`; the source also shows `nîv` before another numeral or noun. The reviewed draft has been expanded to include `nîvekî`.
- **§§149–151, pp.99–100:** `yek` can take `-ê` when used substantively, but before a noun the noun is declined instead; the source gives `ji yekê` and `ji yek hespekî`-type contrasts. Other numeral forms have their own inflected forms, including plural/oblique forms. Avoid reducing all compound-number inflection to a single oversimplified rule.
- **§152, p.100:** `hezar` can take `-î` when used by itself (`ji hezarî)); when followed by another numeral it can be inflected with the following numeral, and when followed by a noun it may remain uninflected. Teach these as separate constructions rather than a blanket `hezar → hezarî` rule.
