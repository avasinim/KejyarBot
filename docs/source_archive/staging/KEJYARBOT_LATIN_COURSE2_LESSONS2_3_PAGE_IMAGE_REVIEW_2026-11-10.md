## Lesson 2 — Common and proper nouns (§§34–37, printed p.51)
- §34 defines nouns as words naming living beings or objects/things; examples include `Zîn`, `pismam`, `şêr`, `hesp`, `post`, and `kursî`.
- §35 distinguishes two broad noun types: `hevenav` and `serenav`.
- §36 explains common nouns as naming members of a class, with examples such as `rêwî`, `hesp`, and `çiya`, plus a wider list of common nouns.
- §37 defines proper nouns as names identifying a particular person, animal, or thing. The source examples include `Sado`, `Bêlan`, and `Herekol`; it also lists `Hevind`, `Sîpan`, `Xabûr`, `Hindistan`, `Hezro`, `Kerevan`, `Zîn`, `Dêrgul`, `Kurdistan`, `Agirî`, `Cemşîd`, and `Ferezende`.
- The reviewed lesson's common/proper noun distinction and source examples align. User-requested `Ferzane`, `Kejyar`, and `Argesh` remain clearly marked as supplemental examples, not source quotations.

## Lesson 3 — Grammatical gender (§§38–45, printed pp.51–53)
- §38 says nouns have two inflectional/grammatical properties: gender and number, and noun variation involves inflection.
- §39 identifies two grammatical genders: masculine and feminine.
- §40 gives a masculine noun list; §41 gives a feminine list. The reviewed lesson preserves the gender classification and now restores two feminine source examples previously omitted from its selected list: `Sînemxan` and `Hezarom`.
- §42 explains that gender pairs can be shown by distinct lexical forms, e.g. `bra/xweh`, `pismam/dotmam`, `bûra/diş`, `kur/keç`, and `hesp/mehîn`; it also shows paired forms built with gender-marking suffixes.
- §43 notes that some nouns are common-gender or may be used for both sexes; in some contexts `mê/nêr` or `dêl/mak` can be prefixed to specify sex. It also notes gender of animal nouns can be marked by determiner/inflection, e.g. `hespa min` versus `kera min`.
- §44 explicitly gives semantic gender shift for `dar`: when meaning a living tree/plant it is feminine, and when meaning dry wood it is masculine. The reviewed draft preserves this as a meaning-dependent grammatical distinction.
- §45 warns that grammatical gender is not always predictable from semantic sex and that some inanimate nouns have conventional gender. The reviewed lesson's warning that grammatical gender does not equal biological sex is aligned.
- §46 begins the following section on noun functions; it is outside the core Lesson 3 scope.
