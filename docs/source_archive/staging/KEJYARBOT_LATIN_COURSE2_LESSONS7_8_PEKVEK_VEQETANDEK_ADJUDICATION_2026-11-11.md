# Course 2 Latin — Lessons 7–8: `pêvek` and `veqetandek` terminology adjudication

**Date:** 2026-11-11  
**Scope:** Lesson 7 (definite forms), Lesson 8 (indefinite forms), and the wider course uses that clarify the terminology.  
**Status:** Editorial terminology ruling for the staging register only; no lesson, quiz, answer-key, glossary, or runtime content is changed.

## Question

Are `pêvek` and `veqetandek` interchangeable labels, synonyms, or terms for different levels of description?

## Evidence in the reviewed course draft

1. Lesson 4 describes `-ek` and `-in` as `pêvekên nebinavkirî` in examples such as `hespek/hespin`. Here `pêvek` is used broadly for a bound form added to a word.
2. Lesson 5 uses `pêvek bistîne` for changes to a noun form under inflection. This is also a broad morphological use; it does not name a determiner category.
3. Lesson 7 explicitly introduces the definite paradigm as `veqetandekên binavkirî` and identifies `-ê, -a, -ên` as its forms. It separately says the definite marker must be distinguished from case inflection, even where both appear in one word form.
4. Lesson 8 explicitly introduces `veqetandekên nebinavkirî` and organizes the indefinite forms by syntactic environment: bare post-nominal forms `-ek/-ek/-in`; forms before a following descriptive modifier `-ekî/-eke/-ine`; pre-adjectival forms `-î/-e/-en`; and the stated oblique forms `-ekî/-ekê/-ina`.
5. Lesson 8's quiz and answer-key rationale also use `veqetandek` as the specific category label, while the explanation for Lesson 8 Q4 uses `pêvekeke raveker` descriptively. That local variation does not establish synonymy.
6. Elsewhere in the course, `pêvek` also describes the comparative suffix `-tir`. This is further evidence that it is the broader morphological word, not a label restricted to the definite/indefinite determiner paradigms.

## Adjudication

For this course's current terminology system, retain a **broader–narrower distinction**, not synonymy:

- **`pêvek`**: broad morphological term for an affix/suffix attached to a base. It may describe several kinds of bound morphology, including inflectional or derivational material. Do not use it as the formal category name for every item in Lessons 7–8.
- **`veqetandek`**: the course's specific label for the definite and indefinite noun-determining forms taught in Lessons 7–8. Keep the compounds `veqetandeka binavkirî` and `veqetandeka nebinavkirî` stable.
- **`pêveka raveker`**: a descriptive phrase meaning an affix/suffix in the environment where a descriptive modifier follows. It should not silently replace the formal label `veqetandeka nebinavkirî` when naming the paradigm.

The practical teaching formulation should make clear that a `veqetandek` in these lessons is realized as a suffix-like bound form, but the broad word `pêvek` and the course's specific grammatical category `veqetandek` are not interchangeable in headings, definitions, or quiz answers.

## Source-page refinement — determiner as grammatical function vs affix as form (2026-11-11)

The earlier adjudication is refined after locating the existing direct page-image verification for §§61–64 (printed pp.62–63) and rereading the source-aligned lesson text:

1. **Source definition (§61):** a `veqetandek` is defined functionally as a small word placed before or after a noun to identify/limit its reference; the source states that determiners can mark gender/number and definite/indefinite reference. §62 then distinguishes definite from indefinite determiners. This is the category/function level, not a claim that every determiner is morphologically a suffix.
2. **Lesson 7 realization:** `-ê/-a/-ên` are presented attached after the noun in the main examples, while the source separately shows shortened definite forms `ê/a/ên` before an adjective when the noun is understood/omitted. The lesson explicitly warns not to confuse these shortened independent forms with the post-nominal attached forms.
3. **Lesson 8 realization:** the indefinite forms are taught in several position- and syntax-conditioned paradigms, including post-nominal forms and forms before a following adjective/modifier, plus a separate oblique environment. Their surface attachment/shape depends on the construction.
4. **`pêvek` remains a morphological/form description:** the course uses it broadly for bound material added to a base (e.g. indefinite `-ek/-in`, noun inflection discussion, and comparative `-tir`). It can describe the suffixal realization of a determiner in a particular construction, but it is not the name of the determiner category itself.

**Refined ruling:** distinguish (a) grammatical function/category `veqetandek`, (b) morphological realization such as a suffix/pêvek in a given construction, and (c) the specific definite/indefinite paradigms. Thus, `pêvek` and `veqetandek` are not interchangeable, but neither should `veqetandek` be defined too narrowly as “a suffix”. Prefer “determiner” as the category gloss; describe the attachment/position of each form separately and preserve the source's shortened pre-adjectival definite forms.
