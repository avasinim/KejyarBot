# Latin Courses 1–2 — source provenance and traceability audit
**Date:** 2026-11-10  
**Branch:** `archive/preservation-audit-2026-11-10`

## Scope and method
Inspected the current reviewed drafts for both Latin-script courses, focusing on their source statements and lesson-level provenance labels. This pass evaluates whether a reviewer can trace a lesson's source basis from the draft itself. It does not independently re-transcribe every page of the source book and does not certify every sentence against the scan.

Primary work cited by Course 2:
Celadet Alî Bedir-Xan, *Elfabêya kurdî & Bingehên gramera kurdmancî*, Nefel, Stockholm, 2002.

The source scan is identified in the project history as:
https://kurdshop.net/content/upload/1/root/celadet-ali-bedir-xan-elfabeugramer-02.pdf

## Course 1 findings
The reviewed draft has 12 lesson-level provenance headings.
- Lessons 3–9 and 11–12 include at least a part/page reference or a clearly scoped source attribution.
- Lessons 4–7 explicitly distinguish supplementary teaching examples from the source's own example lists.
- Lesson 9 explicitly separates direct source examples from supplementary examples.
- Lesson 11 states that the table values are raw counts, not normalized percentages.
- Lessons 1, 2, and 10 have weaker traceability in their current provenance wording. Lesson 1 says facts were rechecked against Part I but gives no page range; Lesson 2 says the lesson was recovered and its sequence retained but gives no precise source locator; Lesson 10 says it is a recovered and corrected draft but does not identify the source passage/page supporting its explanatory synthesis.

These are documentation/traceability gaps, not proof that the lesson content is wrong.

## Course 2 findings
The reviewed draft has a global bibliographic statement and says it uses Part II only. It also records the recovery history of Lessons 1–3 and Lessons 4–12. However, unlike Course 1, it does not include a lesson-level provenance block or page/section locator for each lesson in the draft.

This makes independent sentence-by-sentence review harder, especially for distinctions between:
1. material directly stated by Celadet;
2. editorial teaching explanations or reorganized lesson structure;
3. supplemental examples added for teaching clarity;
4. modern linguistic interpretation.

The global source attribution is useful but is not a substitute for local page/section references.

## Required handling
- Do not label this traceability gap as a factual error.
- Do not add page numbers from memory or infer them from lesson numbering.
- Keep the original source text and source attribution intact.
- Before changing any content, check the relevant printed page and surrounding passage in the scan.
- For each Course 2 lesson, the next provenance pass should add a compact note identifying the printed page/section range and distinguishing source examples from supplemental examples.
- For Course 1 Lessons 1, 2, and 10, add precise page references only after direct source verification. Lesson 10 should remain identified as editorial teaching synthesis if it is not a verbatim source lesson.

## Result
No lesson content or quiz answer was changed in this pass. The main finding is a source-traceability documentation gap: Course 1 has uneven lesson-level provenance, and Course 2 currently relies on a global source note rather than per-lesson locators.
