**Date:** 2026-11-10  
**Branch:** `archive/preservation-audit-2026-11-10`  
**Source:** Celadet Alî Bedir-Xan, *Elfabêya kurdî & Bingehên gramera kurdmancî*, 2002, printed pp.37, 43–44, §§1–4 and 13–16. Direct page images inspected.

## Findings
- §1 defines grammar as the entirety of the rules of correct speaking and writing. Lesson 1's definition is a close match.
- §2 defines Kurmanji as the language of the Kurmanji people and describes speaking, writing, and expressing feelings/thoughts through words. The lesson's summary preserves these points.
- §3 gives the progression from spoken sounds to written letters, letters to syllables, syllables to words, and words to meaningful groups that express a complete thought. It then says discourse/text (`qiset`) is formed by joining groups. The lesson's four-element sequence and its separation of `qiset` from the four basic elements are aligned.
- §4 defines `tîp` as the written sign used to represent a sound. The lesson correctly distinguishes the written sign from the sound itself.
- §13 defines `kît` as a unit formed from one or more letters; the lesson's definition is aligned.
- §14 distinguishes words made from one syllable from words made from multiple syllables; the lesson does not yet teach this finer word classification and does not contradict it.
- §15 defines `bêje` in terms of a thought/idea arising in the mind and notes that it can be expressed through a visible form or through meaning. The lesson's concise definition as a linguistic unit with meaning and function is a pedagogical expansion, not a verbatim source definition. A source-attribution note was added so the conceptual distinction remains visible.
- §16 discusses the parts of discourse and the role of words according to their meaning and function. The lesson's statement that a word is not only a noun is consistent with this broader classification.
- The lesson's earlier sentence describing `qiset` as an outcome of joined groups was too compressed. It has been refined to reflect §3's account: `qiset` is formed by joining groups and is a broader discourse/text unit, not a fifth item in the four-element sequence.
