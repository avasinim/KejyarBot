## 2. Cross-lesson terminology and conceptual boundaries to preserve
1. **Written sign vs sound:** Course 1's `herf` and Course 2's `tîp` are course-specific terms. Preserve the shared concept that a written sign represents a sound; it is not itself the audible sound.
2. **Noun gender vs biological sex:** Keep grammatical gender labels attached to nouns/forms, not people as a claim about biological sex.
3. **Number vs inflection:** Singular/plural and oblique inflection interact, but are not interchangeable concepts. Do not teach `-an` as a universal plural marker detached from case and syntax.
4. **Definiteness vs inflection:** Definite and indefinite markers have their own functions; oblique inflection is a separate grammatical process even when forms co-occur.
5. **Noun modifier vs direct object:** `bireserê navdêrê` (noun modifier) must not be confused with clause-level `bireser` (direct object).
6. **Demonstrative adjective vs pronoun:** `ev/ew` and inflected demonstratives function adjectivally when modifying a noun, and pronominally when standing independently.
7. **Personal-pronoun form vs syntactic role:** Agent, direct-object, and indirect-object roles must be explained in context; surface forms do not map one-to-one to a single role in all constructions.
8. **Possessive adjective vs possessive pronoun:** A possessive form modifying a stated noun differs from a possessive pronoun replacing an understood noun.
9. **Numeral adjective vs numeral pronoun:** Teach by syntactic function—modifying a stated noun versus functioning independently/substantively—not merely by which chapter or table lists the form.
