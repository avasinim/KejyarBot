## Exact quiz item

> Forma bireserî ya `ew` mê çi ye? A) wî B) ew C) wê

Key: **C — `wê`**.

## Lesson evidence

Lesson 10 names the paradigm **Pronavên bireser** and lists `min, te, wî, wê, me, we, wan`. It then explicitly warns that the forms cannot be assigned a fixed clause role in every sentence: in transitive past clauses, a form from this paradigm can function as the inflected agent, while another pronoun is the direct object.

The lesson's own example is:

> `Wê ew dît.`

Here `wê` is the inflected agent and `ew` is the direct object. The earlier E3 recheck likewise confirmed that §86 lists `wê` as the feminine form in the paradigm, while in this clause `wê` is the inflected agent, not the object.

## Finding

The keyed form C is supported as the feminine form in the course's named “pronavên bireser” paradigm. However, the stem `Forma bireserî ya ew mê çi ye?` uses `bireserî` in a way that can be read as asking for the syntactic object form/function. That reading conflicts with the lesson's explicit example where `wê` functions as the agent in a transitive-past clause.

This is a form/paradigm label versus actual clause-role ambiguity, not a proven key-letter error.

## Suggested stem for editorial review

Preferred:

> Di tabloya “pronavên bireser” de, forma mê ya `ew` çi ye?

Alternative:

> Di koma formên pronavên bireser de, forma mê ya `ew` çi ye?

Both preserve key C (`wê`) while making clear that the question concerns paradigm membership, not the role played in a particular sentence.
