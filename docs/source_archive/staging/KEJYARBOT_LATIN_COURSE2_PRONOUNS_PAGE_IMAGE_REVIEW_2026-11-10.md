# Course 2 — Pronoun lessons page-image review
**Date:** 2026-11-10  
**Branch:** `archive/preservation-audit-2026-11-10`  
**Source:** Celadet Alî Bedir-Xan, *Elfabêya kurdî & Bingehên gramera kurdmancî*, 2002, printed pp.71–88, §§72–118. Page images inspected directly.

## Scope and findings

### Lesson 9 — Personal pronouns (pp.71–76; §§72–85)
- §72 defines pronouns as words replacing nouns and notes they may take over noun functions.
- §73 distinguishes masculine, feminine, and neuter pronouns. The draft's explanation that `wî` and `wê` are masculine/feminine and that other personal forms are treated as neuter follows the source's inventory, but must not imply that the pronoun `ew` has only one referent or gender in all contexts.
- §74 lists six pronoun classes: personal, demonstrative, relative, interrogative, indefinite, and possessive. The draft divides this inventory across Lessons 9–11; the cross-reference should remain visible so the taxonomy is not mistaken for a new competing classification.
- §§75–77 give personal pronoun forms and distinguish subject and object forms. The source's second-person plural form is `hon`, not `hûn`; the draft correctly retains `hon`.
- §§78–85 cover transitive verbs and changes in pronoun roles, including subject/object order and reflexive `xwe`. The source examples support the draft's warning not to label `min/te/wî/wê/me/we/wan` as always subject or always object.

### Lesson 10 — Object, reflexive, reciprocal, and compound pronouns (pp.76–80; §§86–93)
- §86 lists object forms `min, te, wî, wê, me, we, wan`; the draft's inventory is aligned. In transitive past constructions, roles can switch; indirect-object examples do not necessarily follow the same switch. The draft already distinguishes this.
- §87 states `xwe` is neuter and can refer across both genders and both numbers. The source also gives its core reflexive use where subject and object refer to the same participant.
- §88 is an important qualification: `xwe` can also occur in coordinated subject phrases, e.g. `Ez û birayê xwe em hatin` and `Min û birayê xwe me got`. The reviewed draft was updated to include these examples and to avoid reducing every use of `xwe` to a single same-subject/object rule.
- §89 gives neuter singular `ê`; it does not replace plural `wan`. The draft's singular/plural contrast is aligned.
- §90 describes reciprocal `hev/êk` for two or three people together. The reviewed draft's previous broad phrase "two or more" was narrowed to "two or three" to match the source wording more closely.
- §§91–93 list compound forms `jê, lê, pê, tê` and `jev/jêk, lev/lêk, pev/pêk, tev/têk`. The source's warning against repeating a preposition is preserved by the draft's `pê ve` example.

### Lesson 11 — Demonstrative, relative, interrogative, indefinite, and possessive pronouns (pp.81–88; §§94–118)
- §§94–96 distinguish demonstrative forms `ev/ew` (near/far) from `yê/ya/yên` (without proximity distinction). The draft's two-group account is aligned.
- §97 states `yê/ya/yên` are subjects in their relative construction and are not direct objects; the relative `ko` follows. The draft's rule and examples match.
- §§99–100: `ko` as a relative pronoun can be subject or direct object in its relative clause; the same spelling also occurs as a conjunction, including a conditional meaning comparable to "heke". The draft distinguishes these functions.
- §§101–103: interrogative pronouns include `kî/kê/çi/çî` and `kîjik/kîjan`. The source distinguishes subject/object forms for `kî/kê/çi/çî` and says these forms are not declined; `kîjik/kîjan` behave like nouns and can be declined. The reviewed draft was updated to state these distinctions.
- §§104–105: `çend` asks about quantity and may itself be declined when used as a pronoun; `kû` asks about place. These details are now explicit in the reviewed draft.
- §§106–113: indefinite pronouns are varied and their forms depend on syntactic context. The draft's selected list is a teaching subset, not an exhaustive reproduction.
- §§114–118: possessive pronouns are formed with demonstrative and personal pronouns. The source distinguishes near forms `vî/vê/van` from far forms `wî/wê/wan`; the draft's selected table should remain explicitly marked as a subset and retain that proximity contrast.

## Reviewed-draft changes
Only the reviewed draft on the archive branch was updated, after fetching its current blob SHA and fetching it again after the update:
1. Lesson 10 now includes the §88 coordinated `xwe` examples and avoids an over-simple reflexive-only account.
2. Reciprocal pronouns are scoped to two or three people, matching §90.
3. Lesson 11 now explains the non-declension of `kî/kê/çi/çî`, the noun-like inflection of `kîjik/kîjan`, and the quantity/place roles of `çend/kû`.
