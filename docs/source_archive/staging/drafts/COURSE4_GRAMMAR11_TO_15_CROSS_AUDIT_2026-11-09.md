## Lesson 11 — bound/clitic pronouns
- Strong point: distinguishes independent pronouns from clitics/enclitics and warns that identical surface forms (-م، -ت، -ی، -مان، -تان، -یان) do not guarantee identical grammatical function.
- Preserve the distinction between nominal possession (e.g. «کتێبەکەم») and the clitic marking an agent in past transitive constructions.
- The preposition-hosted clitic claim is appropriately qualified; require actual examples per preposition rather than extrapolating a paradigm.
- Cross-reference Lesson 10 (independent personal pronouns) and Lesson 12 (agent marking/ergative alignment).
- Terminology control: clitic/enclitic is not synonymous with suffix/affix. A clitic is prosodically/structurally dependent; an affix is morphologically attached within a word.

## Lesson 12 — grammatical function of clitics
- Core contrast is useful: possession vs agent marking in past transitive clauses, with the clause context determining analysis.
- Keep ergative alignment scoped to the relevant Sorani past transitive constructions; do not generalize to every tense, verb or Kurdish variety.
- In «ئەوان وانەکەیان خوێند», explicitly maintain the two possible readings already recorded: agent marking in a past transitive clause vs possessive reading in an appropriate context. Add punctuation/contextual framing to the teaching example so ambiguity is not mistaken for a single unambiguous parse.
- Distinguish agent (بکەر), patient/object (بەرکار), and possessor (خاوەندار). The clitic is not automatically an object marker.
- Cross-reference Lesson 4 for grammatical number and Lesson 10 for independent pronouns, but do not equate verbal agreement with an independent pronoun.

## Lesson 13 — verb morphology
- Preserve the three-way distinction: infinitive (چاوگ), lexical root (ڕەگ), and inflectional stem (بنج). Present and past stems may differ; they are not mechanically extracted from every infinitive by one rule.
- The «دەـ» forms are correctly treated as context-sensitive, not a universal future marker.
- Past tense forms (simple, progressive/habitual, perfect, pluperfect) should be analyzed as paradigmatic constructions; do not assume every segment such as «ـووە» or «بوو» is simply a suffix.
- Keep person/number agreement on the verb distinct from independent pronouns and nominal possessive clitics.
- Maintain distinction between affixes and clitics; do not use “پاشگر” as a blanket label for every attached element.
- Cross-reference Lessons 11–12 for clitic/ergative functions and Lesson 15 for past-tense paradigms.

## Lesson 14 — noun/verb complements and clause functions
- Important distinction: internal relations inside a noun phrase (head, ezafe linker, modifier/complement) are separate from the phrase's external clause role (subject, object, etc.).
- In «گەڵای دارەکە», «گەڵا» is the nominal head and «دارەکە» is an ezafe-linked dependent; the whole noun phrase can function as subject. Do not label the dependent itself “subject complement”.
- In «دۆی ترش», «ترش» is an adjective with the internal noun-phrase role of modifier; adjective as word class and modifier as phrase function are separate levels.
- The term «تەواوکەری بکەر» risks confusion with subject complement/predicate complement in other grammatical traditions. Define the exact intended meaning or replace with a more transparent label; do not silently conflate it with subject, subject complement, or nominal complement.
- Direct object, prepositional complement, and adjunct/adverbial must be distinguished by verb selection, semantic role and construction. The diagnostic “چی؟/کێ؟” is helpful but not decisive alone.
- «بە فڕۆکە» (instrument), «بۆ یارییەکە» (goal/purpose), «لە قوتابخانە» (location) need contextual function labels; do not classify every prepositional phrase uniformly.
- Cross-reference Lessons 3 and 6 for word class vs syntactic function and Lesson 5 for noun phrase structure.

## Lesson 15 — past tense/aspect
- Preserve the contrast between simple past, past progressive/habitual, perfect and pluperfect; their form and discourse interpretation are not interchangeable.
- “Near/far past” should remain qualified as a pedagogical label from some teaching traditions, not a universal semantic rule.
- «دەخوێند» can express ongoing, habitual or repeated past action depending on context; avoid reducing it to only one reading.
- The past perfect/perfect forms «خوێندووە» and «خوێندبوو» require paradigm-based analysis; avoid treating «ـووە» or «بوو» as a uniform suffix across all verbs.
- The heuristic infinitive-minus-ن is not exceptionless; retain the lesson's caution about stem alternations and irregular forms.
- Negative forms (نەخوێند، نەدەخوێند، نەخوێندووە، نەخوێندبوو) should be taught as paradigmatic forms with morpheme order checked per construction.
- Cross-reference Lesson 12 for agent marking in past transitive clauses and Lesson 13 for past stem formation.

## Cross-lesson consistency register
1. Independent pronoun ≠ clitic pronoun ≠ verbal person/number marker.
2. Possessive clitic ≠ agent clitic in past transitive clauses; same shape does not mean same function.
3. Agent/bekar ≠ object/bərkar ≠ possessor.
4. Root (ڕەگ) ≠ stem (بنج) ≠ infinitive (چاوگ).
5. Affix/suffix ≠ clitic/enclitic.
6. Word class (noun/adjective/adverb) ≠ syntactic function (subject/object/modifier/adjunct/complement).
7. Internal noun-phrase dependency ≠ external clause function of the whole phrase.
8. Tense and aspect labels must be kept distinct, with dialect and pedagogical-framework scope recorded.
