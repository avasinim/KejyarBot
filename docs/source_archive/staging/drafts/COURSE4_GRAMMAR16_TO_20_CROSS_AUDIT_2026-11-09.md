## Lesson 16 — present stem and non-past forms
1. Preserve the distinction among infinitive (چاوگ), lexical root (ڕەگ) and present stem (بنجی ئێستا). These are not interchangeable labels.
2. The forms «خواردن/خۆ»، «کڕین/کڕ»، «کردن/کە» and «چوون/چ» are useful paradigm examples, but do not turn one extraction procedure into a universal rule. Verify each verb's paradigm, including stem alternations and irregulars.
3. «دەـ» is a form marker in the lesson's analysis; it does not by itself mean future. Present, habitual and future readings are context-dependent.
4. Distinguish grammatical form from temporal interpretation: «ئێستا دەچم» vs «بەیانی دەچم».
5. The negative «ناـ» contrasts with past «نەـ» and imperative «مەـ»; keep the three in separate paradigms.
6. The person/number endings listed in the appendix are illustrative, not a universal invariant suffix table.

## Lesson 17 — imperative
1. Imperative is a mood (ڕەوش), not a tense or future time. Keep mood, tense and aspect as separate grammatical dimensions.
2. Forms such as «بنووسە/بنووسن» and «بگرە/بگرن» are useful, but segmentation must be verb-specific. Do not treat every final «ە» as the same morpheme.
3. The lesson correctly distinguishes «بکە», where «ە» belongs to the present stem «کە», from «بگرە», where the final segment is analyzed as part of the imperative form.
4. Irregular forms such as «بڕۆ»، «بخۆ»، «بڵێ»، «وەرە» and «بە» must be taught as attested paradigmatic forms, not generated mechanically from a single rule.
5. «مەـ» is the negative imperative marker in the cited examples; keep it separate from «ناـ» non-past negation and «نەـ» past negation.
6. A free pronoun («تۆ/ئێوە») and imperative morphology on the verb are distinct elements. Do not label every ending as a clitic pronoun without evidence.

## Lesson 18 — active and passive voice
1. Keep voice (چالاک/نەچالاک) separate from ergative alignment. Voice concerns clause construction; alignment concerns how grammatical relations are marked.
2. The active/passive pair «مامۆستا وانەکەی نووسی» / «وانەکە نووسرا» is pedagogically useful, but the clitic «ـی» in the first example requires contextual disambiguation from possession. Cross-reference Lessons 11–12.
3. Do not claim that every intransitive verb can form a passive; the lesson correctly notes the lack of a direct object in many intransitive clauses.
4. The example «وانەکە لەلایەن مامۆستا نووسرا» should remain a teaching example pending dialect-specific source verification; do not generalize «لەلایەن» as a universal passive-agent construction across Sorani varieties.
5. The lesson's appendix explicitly states no external source/page mapping is available and prior references to a rejected file must not be reused. Preserve this provenance note.

## Lesson 19 — adverb and adverbial function
1. The core distinction is strong: هاوەڵکار (adverb) is a word class; هاوەڵکاری (adverbial) is a syntactic function.
2. Adverbial function can be realized by a single adverb, a prepositional phrase or a noun phrase; do not call every adverbial constituent an adverb.
3. The examples «پار» (single word) and «لە هاویندا» / «لەژێر کەپرەکەدا» (phrases) should be explicitly labeled by both category and function.
4. Adverbs may modify verbs, adjectives, other adverbs or clauses; keep this wider scope.
5. The questions «کەی؟» and «لە کوێ؟» are diagnostics, not decisive tests. Some adverbials are selected/required by the predicate; not all are optional adjuncts.

## Lesson 20 — derived adverbs with «بەـ»
1. Distinguish the derivational prefix «بەـ» inside a lexicalized word from the independent preposition «بە» introducing a prepositional phrase.
2. «بەخێرایی» and «بەپەلە» are treated as derived adverbs in the lesson; preserve the lexical and orthographic analysis, but verify each item as a word in a dictionary/corpus before extending the pattern.
3. «بە ئۆتۆمبێل» and «بە پێنووس» are prepositional phrases, not one-word derivatives.
4. Not every form beginning with «بە» is prefixed. Orthographic adjacency alone does not prove morphological prefixation.
5. English «-ly» is not a fully equivalent suffix to Kurdish «بەـ»; keep the comparison illustrative and limited.
6. The English gloss of «hardly» should remain «بە دەگمەن/نزیکەی هیچ», not «بەسەختی»; this is a useful lexical warning.
