## 2. Integrated conceptual architecture: eight distinct layers
1. Clause and sentence structure (L37–38, L46): identify clauses and their relations; classify independent/dependent and main/subordinate at the correct analytical level.
2. Predicate and argument structure (L39–41): identify predicate, valency, arguments, and lexical construction; lexical structure is not the same as transitivity.
3. Grammatical functions (L39, L42, L44–45): Subject and Object are syntactic functions, not semantic roles or positions.
4. Semantic roles (L39, L41–44): Agent, Patient/Theme, Recipient/Goal are semantic relations, not interchangeable with Subject/Object.
5. Morphology and clitic hosting (L42–43): clitic status, host, features, function and semantic role are separate diagnostics.
6. Voice (L40, L44): passive is a constructional/grammatical voice relation, distinct from transitivity and intransitivity.
7. Information structure and constituent order (L45): Object Fronting, left dislocation, Topic, Topicalization, Focus and Given/New must remain distinct.
8. Clause linkage and sentence type (L37–38, L46): coordination and subordination relations determine Compound, Complex and Compound-complex classifications; conjunction word class is not the same as clause relation.

## 3. Lesson sequence and dependency checks

### 39 — Object and grammatical function
Object must be diagnosed by its syntactic relation to the predicate, not only by affectedness. Direct Object, Recipient/Goal, complement and adjunct need distinct diagnostics. The example «من کتێبێکم بە هاوڕێکەم دا» needs an explicit analysis of Theme and Recipient and the status of the prepositional phrase.

### 40 — Transitivity and valency
Separate lexical valency from overt realization or omission of arguments. Object omission does not automatically make a predicate intransitive. Passive is not intransitivity; causative/inchoative alternation is not passive. This lesson supplies the argument-structure basis for L41 and L44.

### 41 — Compound verbs and complex predicates
Simplex/derived/compound lexical verbs, Light Verb Constructions and broader Complex Predicates require explicit framework criteria. They are not synonyms, and orthographic spacing is not a diagnostic. Examples such as «شەڕی کرد»، «قسەی کرد»، «سوێندم خوارد» need syntactic and argument-structure analysis. Keep lexical classification separate from transitivity.

### 42 — Clitics and Object function
Clitic is a morphological/prosodic status, not a grammatical function. NP-internal possessive marking differs from a clause-level Object; Subject, Agent, Object, possessor and agreement must be separately identified. «من تۆم بینی» needs explicit Sorani past-transitive/ergative analysis and must not be labeled by suffix shape alone.

### 43 — Clitics in transitive clauses
The lesson title promises object clitics, but examples include independent pronoun Objects, possessive marking, imperative objects and intransitive agreement. A sufficiently demonstrated object-clitic paradigm is not yet established. Strings without full clauses, dialect, intended reading and segmentation remain unverified. «من دەچم ... ئەوان دەچن» is intransitive agreement, not Object clitic marking.

### 44 — Passive and Subject
In a canonical passive, the former Object (often Patient/Theme) may be promoted to Subject-like function; Agent is demoted, optionally omitted or obliquely expressed. Subject is a grammatical function; Agent is a semantic role. Define passive morphosyntactically, not as a required discourse-focus shift. Distinguish passive from middle, anticausative, resultative, reflexive and lexical intransitive constructions. Sorani passive morphology, agreement, aspect/tense, Agent phrase and all example naturalness require dialect-specific validation.

### 45 — Word order and information structure
Grammatical function is not linear position. Object Fronting is a positional/syntactic description; Topicalization is a discourse operation; left dislocation is a separate construction; Focus is an information-structural relation. Topic is not Subject or necessarily Given; Focus is not Object or necessarily New. SOV is a broad Sorani tendency, not an exceptionless rule. Clitic morphology and prosody must be analyzed before word order examples are used as proof of grammatical function.

### 46 — Simple, Compound, Complex and Compound-complex sentences
The expanded draft improves the owner source by separating Compound (two or more coordinated independent clauses), Complex (main clause plus at least one subordinate clause), and Compound-complex (at least two coordinated independent clauses plus at least one subordinate clause). It also distinguishes Clause from Sentence and Independent/Dependent from Main/Subordinate. These are valuable improvements, but remain proposals until approved. The compound-complex example's naturalness, clause boundaries, conjunction roles and subordinate attachment require validation. The draft uses «بەرڕستە» as the main term while retaining «ژێرەڕستە» as a prior alternative; owner decision remains open.

## 5. Critical cross-lesson safeguards
1. Subject vs Agent: never use the two as interchangeable labels, especially in passive and Sorani past-transitive examples.
2. Object vs semantic role: Object is syntactic; Patient/Theme/Recipient are semantic roles.
3. Clitic taxonomy: distinguish possessive clitics, Agent-indexing/ergative clitics, argument clitics and agreement; do not infer function from suffix form alone.
4. Transitivity vs voice: passive clauses are not to be defined as intransitive by default; omission of an argument is not a voice diagnostic.
5. Lexical construction vs valency: compound/light-verb classification does not decide transitivity.
6. Passive vs fronting: passive promotion changes grammatical function in the construction; fronting/topicalization can move an Object without changing its function.
7. Topic/Focus vs syntax: Topic is not Subject, Focus is not Object, and Given/New is another axis.
8. Clause relation vs conjunction word: a conjunction/subordinator is a linking item; the clauses and their relation must be independently identified.
9. Independent/Dependent vs Main/Subordinate: do not collapse structural independence with hierarchical relation labels.
10. Dialect verification: L42–45 and parts of L46 contain forms that require target-dialect, morphology, prosody and naturalness judgments before they are scored.
