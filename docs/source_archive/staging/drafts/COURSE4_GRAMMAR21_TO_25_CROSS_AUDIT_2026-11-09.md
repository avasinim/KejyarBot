## Lesson 21 — adverbial material in compound verbs
1. Preserve the lesson's caution: a spatial-looking element plus a verb does not automatically prove an adverb + verb compound. Analyze lexicalization, stress/word boundary, meaning and dictionary evidence.
2. Forms «ژێرکەوتن»، «بەرکەوتن» and «دەرکردن» are candidates requiring lexicographic verification; the lesson itself appropriately marks the evidence as limited.
3. In «پیاوەکە بەرز قسە دەکات» and «دەرگاکە توند داخە», «بەرز» and «توند» are adjectives by lexical class but have adverbial/manner function in context.
4. «ئەو پێش کەوت» and «ئەو دەر بە دەر گەڕا» should be analyzed as phrases/constructions, not automatically compound verbs.
5. Keep phrase sequence (adverb + phrase + verb) separate from lexical compound formation.

## Lesson 22 — prepositions
1. The independent prepositions «لە»، «بە»، «بۆ» are correctly distinguished from bound material such as «ـە» in «چوومە بازاڕ» and «دەبێتە مامۆستا».
2. A prepositional phrase is not automatically an indirect object. Its clause function depends on predicate selection, semantic role and context.
3. «بە» has multiple roles (instrument, comitative, manner, cause, etc.); avoid one-to-one translation into Persian.
4. «لە» can mark location, source or separation; «بۆ» can mark goal, direction, beneficiary or purpose. Preserve contextual readings.
5. The example «کەژیار دەستی سابوون دەشوات» is flagged as needing clearer wording; use the better-formed examples already in the source without erasing the original.
6. Cross-reference Lesson 20: independent preposition «بە» must be distinguished from derivational prefix «بەـ».

## Lesson 23 — conjunctions
1. «و» links parallel units (words, phrases or clauses); linking two noun phrases does not create a compound word.
2. «یان» marks alternatives; «نە... نە» expresses coordinated negation in the given example.
3. «ـیش» in «منیش هاتم» is an additive/focus particle, not a conjunction. Keep the bound-particle analysis distinct from independent conjunctions.
4. «بەڵام» expresses contrast; «بەڵکو»، «کە»، «تا» and «هەتا» need construction-specific classification rather than a single undifferentiated conjunction label.
5. «یا» can be multifunctional; classify by its actual clause role and construction, not spelling alone. This is especially important because it also appears in Lesson 24's vocative material.

## Lesson 24 — vocative markers
1. Preserve the supplied forms and positional distinction: «یا» and «ئەی» before the addressee; «ا» after the addressed form in the example «خوایا».
2. Vocative marking addresses an interlocutor; conjunctions connect syntactic units. The functions are distinct.
3. Since «یا» occurs in more than one supplied classification, avoid asserting a universal part-of-speech label without sentence context.
4. Examples added for teaching expansion should remain identified as pedagogical extensions, not corpus evidence or universal dialect evidence.

## Lesson 25 — ezafe and the form «ی»
1. Ezafe is a grammatical relation between a nominal head and a following dependent; «ی» is one possible overt form, not an obligatory marker in every construction.
2. Preserve open vs closed ezafe from Lesson 6: «نامەی درێژ» vs «نامە درێژەکە». Absence of a separately written «ی» does not prove absence of an ezafe relation.
3. Distinguish ezafe «ی» from possessive clitic «ـی» in examples such as «گوڵەکەی چاند» (Lesson 14).
4. The Ardalān/Sanandaj note (e.g. «چەم سیروان»، «پارک ئاشتی») is explicitly variety-scoped and must not be generalized to all Sorani.
5. The supplied vowel-final examples («چرای ماڵ»، «چرای خامۆش»، «دەسەی گوڵ»، «پەنجەرەی شکاو») are retained as source-provided examples, not as a universal phonological rule.
6. The eight ezafe relation labels supplied («تەوسیفی، موڵکی، تایبەتی، قەرزی، بەیانی، فەرزەنی، تەوزیحی، تەشبیهی») are preserved as a list only; definitions and examples are not invented where source detail is absent.
