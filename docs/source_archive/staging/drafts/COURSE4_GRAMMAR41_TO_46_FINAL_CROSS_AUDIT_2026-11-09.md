## Lesson 41 — simple and compound verbs; relation to objects
1. The lesson correctly separates morphological structure (simple vs compound verb) from syntactic valency/transitivity (transitive vs intransitive). These are independent classification axes.
2. The nominal element in an NV+LV compound predicate is not automatically a direct object. Distinguish lexicalized verbal constituent from an external object NP.
3. Examples «شەڕکردن»، «قسەکردن»، «ئیشکردن»، «هەوڵدان» need lexeme- and construction-level treatment; spacing alone cannot determine whether the nominal element is internal to the compound.
4. «سوێندێکی گەورەی خوارد» may require a distinct constructional analysis from the lexicalized infinitive «سوێندخواردن». Do not force one analysis onto both.
5. «نانەکە خوارد» vs «نانخواردن» must be interpreted by syntax, lexicalization and context. Do not infer compoundhood from adjacency.
6. The agent noun examples (ڕاکەر، ئیشکەر، شەڕکەر، نانخۆر) are nouns, not finite verbs; distinguish word-formation from syntactic role in a sentence.
7. The draft's numbering skips sections and some examples need sentence-level naturalness/source validation; preserve original material and append editorial notes only.

## Lesson 42 — clitic pronouns and object function
1. Preserve the distinction among clitic form, grammatical function of the whole NP, and agreement morphology on the verb.
2. In «کتێبەکەم خوێندەوە», the whole NP «کتێبەکەم» can be the object; «ـم» inside it marks possession, not the object function itself.
3. Independent pronoun object examples («من ئەو دەناسم», «ئەوان ئێمە دەناسن») are not examples of object clitics. The lesson must not claim it has demonstrated clitic object marking unless a validated clitic example is added.
4. The clitic set ـم، ـت، ـی، ـمان، ـتان، ـیان is not a uniform object-pronoun paradigm; function depends on host, construction, tense/aspect, dialect and argument structure.
5. Agent marking in relevant past transitive constructions (cross-reference Lessons 11–12) must be distinguished from object clitics, possessive clitics and verbal agreement.
6. Examples «دەمناسێت»، «دەتناسێت» and transcript forms «گرتمن، ناردمیت، گرتتن، گرتتانن، گرتمی، گرتنی» remain unverified. Preserve them as pending data; do not manufacture morphological parses.
7. The lesson is broad introductory material and overlaps substantially with Lesson 43. Define separate learning outcomes or consolidate only with explicit owner approval.

## Lesson 44 — active/passive; grammatical subject vs Agent
1. Preserve grammatical Subject and semantic Agent as separate notions. In a passive clause, the patient/theme can be grammatical Subject while the Agent is omitted or expressed obliquely.
2. The active/passive pairs «مامۆستا وانەکەی نووسی → وانەکە نووسرا», «پۆلیس دزەکەی گرت → دزەکە گیرا», «کەژیار دەرگاکەی کردەوە → دەرگاکە کرایەوە» are pedagogical examples; each form must be checked for standard Sorani morphology and naturalness.
3. Do not present a single mechanical passive conversion rule as applying to every transitive verb. Passive availability and form are lexeme-, construction- and dialect-sensitive.
4. Passive is not synonymous with intransitive. «کەژیار هات» is intransitive, not passive; retain this contrast.
5. The Agent phrase with «لەلایەن» needs variety- and register-specific validation; do not universalize it as the only or obligatory Agent expression.
6. Keep passive Subject distinct from Agent and from the original active Object; align terminology with Lessons 18 and 33.

## Lesson 45 — word order, object fronting, topic and focus
1. The lesson correctly distinguishes grammatical function from linear position. Initial position alone does not identify Subject.
2. SOV is a useful broad description for many Sorani clauses, not an exceptionless rule. Account for information structure, clause type, clitics, prosody and dialect.
3. Object fronting, topicalization and focus are related but distinct. Not every fronted constituent is an object; not every topic is a moved object; focus is not reducible to position.
4. «ئەم کتێبە، من خوێندمەوە» needs prosodic and discourse context to verify its naturalness and exact interpretation. Preserve as a pedagogical candidate, not a universal template.
5. In «کەژیار ئاڕگەشی بینی» vs «ئاڕگەش کەژیاری بینی», the grammatical roles and event participants change; this is not merely a focus/word-order alternation. The lesson correctly flags this and should retain the contrast.
6. Given/new information is discourse-dependent; a word is not inherently given or new outside context.
7. Cross-reference Lessons 32–34 and 39–40: identify constituent, grammatical function, verb valency and discourse role separately.

## Lesson 46 — simple, compound, complex and compound-complex sentences
1. The draft's core clause-based classification is coherent: simple = one independent clause; compound = two or more coordinated independent clauses; complex = a main clause plus at least one subordinate clause; compound-complex = two or more independent clauses plus at least one subordinate clause.
2. Preserve «ڕستە» (sentence) vs «پێڕستە» (clause), and independent vs subordinate clause. Count clause relations, not merely words or verb forms.
3. Distinguish coordination from subordination; conjunction labels must be construction-sensitive. «کە» is multifunctional and cannot be assigned one fixed class in all contexts.
4. Distinguish syntactic type of subordinate clause (e.g. complement, relative, adverbial) from semantic relation (time, cause, condition, concession). Do not classify only by the opening marker.
5. The terms «بەرڕستە» and «ژێرەڕستە» are not yet owner-approved as a unified course terminology. Keep the draft's stated proposal provisional and cross-reference Lessons 37–38.
6. Lesson 46 explicitly proposes a distinction for English Compound vs Complex terminology but states it is not the owner's final decision. Keep this as a terminology decision requiring approval, not as a silently finalized standard.
7. The compound-complex example is explicitly pedagogical and requires naturalness validation in the course's target Sorani variety.
8. The table and quiz should be checked against clause boundaries in every example; do not let a quiz answer assume a classification not demonstrated by the clause analysis.
