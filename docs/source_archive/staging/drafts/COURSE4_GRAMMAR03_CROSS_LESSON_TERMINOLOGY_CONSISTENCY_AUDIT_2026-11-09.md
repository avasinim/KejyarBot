## Verified cross-lesson alignments

1. **Morphology / syntax boundary.** Lesson 1 defines morphology through word-internal structure and form changes, and syntax through word/group arrangement and relations in clauses/sentences. Lesson 3 should continue to identify noun as a word class and subject/object as syntactic functions; its activities already test that distinction. Keep this contrast explicit whenever Lesson 3 examples are reused.
2. **Word formation vs inflection.** Lesson 3's distinction among simple, derived and compound nouns should remain aligned with the course's derivation/inflection distinction: derivation creates a lexeme/word, inflection expresses grammatical forms of a lexeme. Do not describe plural or definiteness suffixes as derivational word formation.
3. **Noun classification axes.** Lesson 3's common/proper, singular/plural, definite/indefinite, concrete/abstract and collective categories are different classification dimensions. This matches the need elsewhere in the course to avoid treating a semantic class as a grammatical number marker.
4. **Ezafe is not an interfix by default.** Lesson 6 explicitly describes -ی- as a relational/ezafe marker in noun-adjective and nominal constructions, and warns that -ی- alone does not determine word class. Lesson 3's morphological interfix discussion must not reclassify this ezafe/linking marker as a compounding interfix without independent word-formation evidence.
5. **Nominal linker vs compound.** Lesson 6 distinguishes open and closed ezafe constructions and locates definiteness marking over the noun-adjective phrase in relevant constructions. The Lesson 3 appendix should preserve the distinction between an ezafe phrase/linker and a lexical compound. Orthographic adjacency alone is not sufficient evidence of compounding.
6. **Terminology for word formation.** Preserve the course's existing distinctions among بنەما/بناغە (base, with source-specific term noted), مۆرفیم, پاشگر, پێشگر, داڕشتن and لێکدان. Avoid using ناوبەند as a synonym for affix or ezafe: in the Lesson 3 discussion it refers specifically to an interfix analysis.

## Consistency risks requiring controlled treatment

- **«ناوبەند»**: use only for a proposed morphological interfix between lexical bases. Do not apply it to an ordinary conjunction و, ezafe ی, phonological glide, preposition or particle.
- **«لینکەر / پەیوەندیی ئیزافە»**: reserve for the relevant nominal phrase relation; do not merge it with the morphological interfix category.
- **«لێکدراو»**: use for lexical compounds only when supported by lexical meaning, phonological/structural behavior and source evidence. Coordinated phrases and ordinary word pairs should be classified separately.
- **«کۆمەڵ»**: retain as a semantic/lexical category; do not equate it with morphological plural.
- **«تاک/کۆ» and «ناسراو/نەناسراو»**: keep as separate grammatical axes; neither should be used as a synonym for common/proper.
- **Dialect scope**: a claim sourced from Silemani Central Kurdish or another Central Kurdish variety must be labeled with that scope. Do not silently generalize it to all Sorani varieties.
- **English labels**: retain consistent mappings: morphology = مۆرفۆلۆژی; syntax = سینتاکس; derivation = داڕشتن; compounding = لێکدان/وشەی لێکدراو; interfix = ناوبەند; ezafe = ئیزافە. Any alternative Kurdish term needs an explicit first-use equivalence, not silent alternation.
