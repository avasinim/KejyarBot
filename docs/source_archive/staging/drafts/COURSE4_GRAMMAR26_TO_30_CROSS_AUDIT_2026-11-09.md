## Lesson 26 — Sorani discourse particles
1. Keep the scope narrow: «پارتیکڵی گفتوگۆیی» (discourse particle) is not a catch-all label for every item called particle in morphology or syntax.
2. The source Radnia (2022) is specifically about Sorani, but its author notes the lack of a representative Kurdish conversational corpus and relies on observations. Preserve this evidence limitation; do not call the examples corpus-validated or universal.
3. A form such as «ئەوە» must be classified in context: discourse particle «Awa» in one construction, demonstrative pronoun/determiner in others.
4. «مەگەم» changes pragmatic force according to the polarity and interpretation of the clause; negation belongs to the clause/verb, not to the particle itself.
5. Distinguish free discourse particles from bound «ـیش» (additive/emphatic clitic) and from verbal particles «داـ/هەڵـ». Shared label particle does not mean shared grammatical class or morphology.
6. Particle position and co-occurrence patterns are construction-specific. The documented combinations are not a universal ordering rule.

## Lesson 27 — interjections
1. Keep three levels separate: interjection (word/discourse form), exclamative sentence/utterance (clause or discourse type), and vocative (addressing an addressee).
2. Preserve Ameka's expressive, conative and phatic functional distinction as a framework, not as an exclusive one-function-per-form classification.
3. A single form can change function with context, intonation and addressee; spelling alone cannot decide whether «ئەی»، «ئەرێ»، «خۆ»، «جا» or «دەی» is an interjection, vocative or discourse particle.
4. McCarus (2009) is specifically described as Sulaimani Sorani; Fossum (1919) is historical comparative evidence; Taha (2022) concerns intonation/exclamatives. Do not combine their lists into a single timeless, dialect-neutral inventory.
5. Mark teaching expansions separately from source-attested examples. Avoid claiming contemporary corpus frequency or uniform meanings where no corpus evidence is supplied.

## Lesson 28 — infinitive and past-stem groups
1. Infinitive (چاوگ) is the lexical citation form and is distinct from finite/personal forms such as «خواردم». Preserve the distinction from Lesson 13 and Lesson 16.
2. The five groups are based on the final segment of the past stem after separating the infinitive marker, not on the letter immediately before final «ن» in the written infinitive.
3. Keep the five endings distinct: -î (e.g. «فڕیـ»), -û («بووـ»), -ā/-a («هێناـ»), -d («خوێندـ/بردـ») and -t («هاتـ/گرتـ»). These classify past stems, not five types of infinitive as a lexical category.
4. The infinitive ending -(i)n is a useful generalization for Sorani, but stem alternations and irregular paradigms mean no single mechanical conversion rule should be imposed.
5. Do not conflate past stem with past tense: the label «بنجی ڕابردوو» names the stem used in relevant forms; it does not mean the stem itself carries tense.
6. Goran/Hawrami examples are comparative only and must not be transferred into the Sorani paradigm.

## Lesson 29 — simple and derived infinitives
1. Preserve the framework qualification: «simple» vs «non-simple» is an analytical classification and is not identical across all morphological theories.
2. Distinguish root (ڕەگ), stem (بنج), derivational affix and infinitive marker. In «سووتاندن», for example, the derivational material and final infinitive marker must not be bundled into one undifferentiated suffix.
3. Keep derivation (new lexeme/lexical meaning) separate from inflection (grammatical form of the same lexeme).
4. Distinguish derived causative forms such as «مردن → مراندن» and «سووتان → سووتاندن» from particle verbs such as «هەڵگرتن/داگرتن/ڕاگرتن/وەرگرتن». The latter are not automatically suffixal derivations.
5. The function of «ـەوە» is construction- and lexeme-dependent: it may contribute a directional/repetitive aspect or form a distinct lexical meaning. Do not label every occurrence as a derivational suffix.
6. Orthographic joining, spacing or ZWNJ alone does not determine morphological category. Use construction, semantics and paradigmatic behavior.

## Lesson 30 — complex predicates and light verbs
1. Keep the NV + LV analysis distinct from simple derivation and from particle-verb analysis. The source literature uses different frameworks; explicitly name which framework is being applied.
2. A light verb carries inflectional features (tense/aspect, negation, person marking) while the non-verbal element contributes substantial lexical meaning; however, light verbs are not semantically empty.
3. The same verb (e.g. «دان», «کردن», «بوون») may be a lexical/heavy verb in one construction and a light verb in another. Decide by constructional meaning and argument structure, not by the verb lemma alone.
4. NV need not be only a noun: adjective, adverb, prepositional phrase and preverbal particle analyses occur in the cited framework. Label each example by the actual constituent type.
5. «لە دەستدان» and «لە دایک بوون» are PP + verb constructions in Kareem's analysis; do not misclassify the whole PP as a verbal particle.
6. «ڕا + کردن» and «هەڵ + کردن» have competing analyses: Kareem (2016) treats preverbal particles as NV elements in complex predicates in the relevant framework, while Ahmadi (2021) classifies particle verbs separately. Retain both with source attribution; do not force a false consensus.
7. The examples «شەڕکردن»، «ماچکردن»، «بەڵێن دان» and «ئیستقالە دان» require sentence-level evidence before making claims about productivity or argument structure.
