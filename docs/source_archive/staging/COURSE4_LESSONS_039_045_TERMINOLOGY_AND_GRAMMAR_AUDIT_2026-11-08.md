## 3. Lesson-by-lesson findings

### Lesson 39 — بەرکار و ئەرکی ڕێزمانیی ئەو

- The core definition is substantially safer than the oversimplified definition “the thing affected by the action”: it allows grammatical and semantic relations and distinguishes direct from indirect objects.
- The indirect-object section correctly includes a caution that not every phrase introduced by «بە»، «بۆ»، or «لە» is an indirect object. Keep this caution and apply it consistently in examples and answer keys.
- The definition of complement is currently broad (“necessary or additional information”). It risks merging complements with adjuncts. Replace with a constituent/valency-based explanation after the course glossary decision.
- A location phrase such as «لە کتێبخانەدا» should not be labelled a complement merely because it adds information. Determine whether the predicate selects it; otherwise classify it as an adjunct.
- The combined transcript repeats the Lesson 39 title twice at its opening. Remove duplication only in a future edited draft, never in the preserved source transcript.
- Some exercise keys label time, manner, and place phrases as “تەواوکەر”. These labels need review under the complement/adjunct distinction.

### Lesson 40 — کاری تێپەڕ و کاری تێنەپەڕ

- The core distinction should be framed through direct-object valency/argument structure. A transitive verb may occur without an overt object in context (object omission/implicit object) without automatically becoming lexically intransitive.
- The source material's “all body parts participate / do not participate” test is not a valid grammatical criterion. It must not appear as an endorsed rule in the final lesson.
- The source material's use of «مان، تان، یان» as a general test for transitivity is unsafe: clitic forms may encode different grammatical relations depending on tense, construction, dialect, and verb type.
- Passive formation is a useful diagnostic in some constructions, but not an exceptionless test. Its limits must be stated.
- The draft itself notes that intransitive verbs can take complements (e.g. a locative phrase); preserve this distinction and do not equate “takes any phrase” with transitivity.
- The discussion of object omission should distinguish (a) grammatically licensed implicit objects, (b) contextually recoverable objects, and (c) genuinely ungrammatical omission for a given verb sense. Avoid treating one example as a universal rule.
- A citation/attribution to Chomsky appears in the source material without a specific work or precise concept. Do not retain the attribution as authoritative until verified; the linguistic phenomenon can be explained without unsupported attribution.

### Lesson 41 — کردارە سادە و لێکدراوەکان و پەیوەندییان بە بەرکارەوە

- Keep “simple/compound verb” distinct from “simple/compound sentence”. Always use the full term («کرداری سادە/لێکدراو») to prevent category confusion.
- The section on lexicalized compound verbs must distinguish lexical composition from a syntactic phrase consisting of noun + light verb. A visible split between components in a sentence does not, by itself, prove that the first component is a grammatical object.
- The claim that the first component of all such verbs functions as an object is too broad. Analyze each verb construction individually; some first elements are lexical parts, incorporated arguments, or construction-specific components.
- Derivational nouns such as agent nouns (e.g. forms ending in «ـکەر») are not themselves evidence that the source verb is transitive or that its first element is an object. Keep derivation and clause syntax separate.
- Preserve the distinction among Agent («ئەنجامدەری کردار»), grammatical Subject («بکەر»), and Object («بەرکار»).

### Lesson 42 — جێناوی لکاو و ئەرکی بەرکار لە زمانی کوردیدا

- The lesson correctly warns that clitics can have multiple functions, including object, subject/agent-related marking, and possession. This is a key rule and should be repeated in the exercise instructions.
- Do not call every attached personal marker an object clitic. Its function must be diagnosed in the specific clause and verbal construction.
- The staged transcript visibly interleaves a large block of earlier source material on transitivity and clitics with the expanded lesson. Preserve that material and its provenance; separate it into source vs editorial draft before final editing.
- The rule sets for imperative, present/non-past, and past constructions require dialect- and verb-class-specific verification. Do not turn the listed examples into universal rules without checking their morphology and agreement pattern.
- Examples and paradigms need a complete person/number grid and explicit segmentation (verb stem, tense/aspect marker, clitic, agreement marker). Incomplete or corrupted examples should not be used as teaching evidence.

### Lesson 43 — جێناوی لکاو لە کردارە تێپەڕەکاندا

- This lesson appropriately narrows the focus to object clitics in transitive constructions and builds on Lesson 42's multi-function distinction.
- It must explicitly refer back to Lesson 42's diagnostic: identify the clitic's function before calling it an object.
- The draft itself cautions that in some past-tense constructions it may be difficult to decide quickly whether a clitic marks subject/Agent or object. This is essential, not optional; retain it and provide fully analyzed examples.
- Audit all person/number combinations and clitic ordering against one declared Kurdish variety/standard. Do not mix paradigms from different dialects without labelling them.
- Keep grammatical Subject, semantic Agent, and Object as separate analysis columns in tables.

### Lesson 44 — بەرکار لە ڕستەی نەکارادا

- The distinction between grammatical Subject and semantic Agent is correctly foregrounded and should become the standard explanation across Lessons 39–45.
- Avoid defining an active sentence as one where the Subject is always the Agent. Active clauses can have non-agentive subjects (experiencers, themes, causes, etc.).
- In a passive clause, the promoted object may function as grammatical Subject while retaining its semantic role; the Agent may be omitted or expressed in an oblique/by-phrase. Do not state that the Agent is simply deleted in every passive.
- The wording “the person or thing affected by the action” is not a universal definition of passive Subject. Use the grammatical construction and argument mapping, not semantic affectedness alone.
- Examples with «لە لایەن» and other agent phrases need dialect/register validation. The draft already warns that the same phrase may not behave identically across varieties; retain that caution.
- Ensure each active/passive pair preserves tense, aspect, polarity, and intended meaning as far as the language permits.

### Lesson 45 — ڕیزبەندی و گواستنەوەی بەرکار لە ڕستەدا

- The lesson makes a useful distinction among word order, object fronting, topicalization, and focus. Preserve these as separate concepts.
- A constituent's initial position does not prove it is the grammatical Subject. The draft correctly warns against this; apply the warning in all parsing exercises.
- Topicalization does not necessarily involve a direct object; the topicalized constituent may have another grammatical or discourse relation. Keep this explicit.
- Focus is not simply “putting the object first”. It can be expressed through word order, prosody, particles, or context, depending on the variety.
- The default SOV order should be presented as a common/basic pattern, not an exceptionless rule for all Kurdish clauses and dialects.
- Examples need paired neutral, topicalized, and focused contexts; otherwise the labels may be assigned from word order alone.

## 4. Cross-lesson consistency issues requiring resolution

1. **بکەر vs ئەنجامدەری کردار:** retain as distinct grammatical and semantic functions. Do not use them interchangeably.
2. **بەرکار vs تەواوکەر vs هاوەڵکار:** establish a diagnostic for selected complements vs optional adjuncts; avoid using «تەواوکەر» as a catch-all.
3. **جێناوی لکاو:** distinguish object clitics from subject/agent marking and possessive clitics in every paradigm.
4. **کرداری تێپەڕ:** define by object argument structure, not by bodily involvement, suffix acceptance, or a single passive test.
5. **کرداری لێکدراو:** distinguish lexicalized compound verbs, light-verb constructions, and free syntactic phrases.
6. **کارا/نەکارا:** keep voice separate from semantic roles; Agent is not always Subject.
7. **بابەتکردن/جەخت:** keep discourse functions separate from grammatical relations and object fronting.
8. **Dialect scope:** identify the target Kurdish variety for each morphological paradigm and label variants explicitly.
