## 2. Overall finding

The 62-item set is broadly aligned with the current course terminology architecture. No broad systematic drift was found in the lesson labels for noun, gender, number, case, definiteness/indefiniteness, pronouns, or Lesson 12's six adjective classes. Most issues are localized wording risks and two already-open morphology/lexeme queries. The following distinctions must remain explicit:

- grammatical word class vs syntactic function;
- attributive noun modifier vs clause-level object;
- pronoun form vs the role that form takes in a particular clause;
- definiteness/indefiniteness marker vs affix vs case/inflection;
- numeral adjective with an expressed noun vs independent `jmarnav`;
- course label vs the source's wording.

## 3. Item-level findings

### A. Items aligned with the controlled terminology

- L1 Q1–Q5: `gramêr`, `tîp`, `kît`, `bêje`, `komek`, and `qiset` follow the lesson's own four-element hierarchy and its separate treatment of `qiset`. The key's L1 Q4 explanation correctly identifies `hesp` as a meaningful word (`bêje`), not a syllable.
- L2 Q1–Q5: `navdêr`, `hevenav`, and `serenav` are consistent. L2 Q3 includes `rengdêr` as a distractor before the formal Lesson 12 definition; this is anticipatory vocabulary, not a contradictory classification.
- L3 Q1–Q5: `zayend`, `nêr/mê`, and `zayenda rêzimanî` align with Lesson 3's explicit distinction between grammatical and biological gender.
- L4–L5: `mêjer` and `tewang` are consistently kept separate. The key's L5 Q2–Q4 rationales preserve the present-tense object examples and avoid overgeneralizing the transitive-past pattern.
- L7–L8: the definite and indefinite determiner paradigms are distinguished; L8 Q5 explicitly tests that `-ên` and `-ine` do not have the same function. L8 Q6 and Q7 track the lesson's separate pre-adjective and oblique-form sets.
- L9: `forma kirarî` is presented as a morphological pronoun form in the transitive past, not as a claim that the form always performs the subject role. This is consistent with the lesson's own caution.
- L10 Q1 and Q4: `pronavên bireser` and clause-level `bireser` are used in the object-pronoun lesson, with the examples supplying the needed clause context.
- L11 Q3–Q4: the quiz distinguishes `ko` as `pronavê girêkî` in its relative construction from `ko` as `gihanek` in `Min got ko ez nayêm`. This distinction is explicit in the lesson text and answer key.
- L12 Q2, Q4, and Q5: `mezin` as `rengdêra wesfîn`, independent `jmarnav`, and `nîv` as a `jmarnav` example follow the current course taxonomy.

### B. Wording and terminology risks requiring editorial attention

1. **L6 Q3 — bare `bireser` in a noun-modifier lesson.** The question asks `Di “hespê beza” de bireser çi ye?` and the key gives `beza`. Within the example, the intended answer is recoverable, but the item reuses bare `bireser` after the lesson and course register explicitly warn that `bireserê navdêrê` differs from clause-level `bireser`. Future authorized wording should use the full compound or explicitly say “hêmana ku navdêrê rave dike”; no edit is made here.
2. **L8 Q2 — option C's phrase `bê hêmaneke raveker a li pey navdêrê`.** The intended contrast for `hespek hat` is the absence of a following descriptive element. The option is understandable but uses a phrase-level explanation rather than a stable short technical label. Retain its present key; consider parallel wording only in a later authorized quiz edit.
3. **L10 Q2 — `forma bireserî ya ew mê` → `wê`.** This wording needs a Kurmanji grammar/editor ruling. Lesson 9 calls `wê` the feminine `forma kirarî` in its pronoun table; Lesson 10's own example `Wê ew dît` labels `wê` as the inflected agent/subject and `ew` as the direct object. The quiz phrase “forma bireserî” can be read as a grammatical object form, which is not what the Lesson 10 example demonstrates. Do not silently change the key: verify the intended technical sense of `bireserî` in the source and choose a consistent label.
4. **L11 Q3 — abbreviated `forma girêkî`.** The lesson body calls `ko` a `pronavê girêkî`; the quiz shortens this to `forma girêkî`. The answer is still clear, but the full established term would improve continuity. L11 Q4 correctly tests the other function, `gihanek`.
5. **L12 Q1 — adjective definition distractor/answer framing.** The keyed answer “describes a feature/quality of a noun” is the best option among the three, but it remains narrower than the course/source evidence allowing predicative or substantivized adjective use. This is a known assessment limitation, not a wrong key. Preserve the earlier recommendation to qualify the lesson definition and avoid treating “expressed noun” as a universal requirement.
6. **L12 Q1 option C — `kiryarê` vs register `kirar`.** This is the previously recorded unresolved lemma/spelling/inflection discrepancy. It occurs in a distractor, but remains a visible course terminology mismatch. Do not globally replace it pending editorial/source review.
7. **L12 Q3 — `rengdêra jmarîn`.** The quiz option still differs from explanatory `rengdêrê jmarîn` and plural class heading `rengdêrên jmarîn`. The concept/key A are aligned; the morphology is not adjudicated. No normalization is authorized.

### C. Context-sensitive category labels, not automatic contradictions

- L6's `ev keçik` is a noun-attached demonstrative modifier; L11's `ev/ew` may be independent demonstrative pronouns; L12 uses the adopted course class `rengdêrên îşarkî` for the noun-associated use. These labels are compatible only when the phrase/sentence context is kept.
- L11 `çend`, `gelek`, and `hemî` may function pronominally when independent; Lesson 12 includes some of the same forms in adjective subclasses when they modify expressed nouns. The quizzes do not comprehensively assess this boundary. Do not infer one invariant class from the lexical form alone.
- L12 `sê mal` is a numeral-adjective example because the noun is expressed; `jmarnav` is taught as an independent/substantive numeral category. The key's L12 Q3 and Q4 test the intended distinction; Q5 identifies `nîv` as an example of the course's numeral category. Keep the previously documented two-level analysis of `nîv roj`.
- L9 `forma kirarî` and L10 clause roles `kirar/bireser` must not be collapsed. A morphological form label does not by itself assign a fixed syntactic role in every sentence.
