# Latin Courses 1–2 — cross-course terminology alignment pass
**Date:** 2026-11-10  
**Branch:** `archive/preservation-audit-2026-11-10`

## Scope
Compared the reviewed drafts of Latin Course 1 (alphabet) and Latin Course 2 (grammar), focusing on written symbols vs speech sounds, course-specific terminology, and shared conceptual boundaries.

## Findings and changes
1. **Course-specific terms `herf` and `tîp`:** Course 1 consistently names alphabet letters `herf`; Course 2 uses `tîp` in its four-unit account of written/linguistic structure. These terms were not globally replaced. A cross-course note was added to Course 2 Lesson 1 to explain the course convention and preserve the distinction between a written sign and the speech sound it represents.
2. **Course 1 consonant label:** A residual `herfên din` label in Lesson 7 was too vague after the inventory had already been divided into vowels and consonants. It was changed to `herfên dengdar`. Lesson 1's consonant inventory label was likewise verified as `herfên dengdar`.
3. **Sound vs written symbol:** Both courses retain the distinction that a written symbol represents a sound; it is not itself the spoken sound.
4. **Scope boundary:** Course 1 is an alphabet/letter course; Course 2 develops grammar and the four-unit account `tîp → kît → bêje → komek`. The audit did not force Course 1's alphabet terminology into Course 2's grammar framework.

## Other shared boundaries retained
- Written letter/symbol vs audible sound.
- Course 2 noun gender vs biological sex.
- Number vs oblique inflection.
- Definite/indefinite marking vs oblique inflection.
- Noun modifier vs clause-level direct object.
- Demonstrative modifier vs demonstrative pronoun.
- Personal-pronoun form vs syntactic role.
- Possessive adjective vs possessive pronoun.
- Numeral adjective vs the separate `jmarnav` category in the course's adopted classification.
