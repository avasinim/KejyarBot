# Course 4 Lesson 3 — 2025 Sorani corpus study: cross-paper triangulation and methodological audit (2026-11-10)

## Scope
This append-only note adds a 2025 Sorani corpus-based study to the Lesson 3 compounding/interfix evidence file. It checks what the study can support, what it cannot support, and an arithmetic issue in its reported corpus percentages. It does not modify the owner source or runtime JSON.

## Source
Mustafa, Hezha Hamid, & Ali M. Jukil (2025). “Derivation, Compounding, and Borrowing Productivity in Kurdish (Sorani Dialect): A Corpus-based Study.” *Journal of Language Studies*, 9(4), Part 1, 181–193. DOI: 10.25130/Lang.9.4.P1.10.
PDF: https://jls.tu.edu.iq/index.php/jls/article/download/1639/1247/4877

Relevant PDF locations:
- Corpus design: pp. 181–182 (PDF lines 70–83).
- Productivity criteria and barriers: pp. 183–185 (lines 100–189).
- Compounding and linking-tool examples: pp. 187–189 (lines 217–258).
- Results and totals: pp. 191–192 (lines 294–332).

## 1. What this study adds
The authors state that they designed a 3,000-word Sorani corpus using dictionary entries from Nanawazada (2021) and Rohani (2020), selected alphabetically. The dataset focuses on Kurdish content words. They classify derivation, compounding, and borrowing and report the number of words assigned to each process.

The study explicitly treats compounding as combining two free bases, sometimes without a linking tool and sometimes with one. Examples it presents include:
- Without a linking tool: `ڕەشماڵ`, `سەربان`, `دەستنۆێژ` (romanized in the paper).
- With a linking tool: `مانگەشەو`, `گوڵەگەنم`, `دەم‌وچاو`, `ڕەنگاوڕەنگ`, `لەرزوتا`, `گورچوگۆڵ`.
- Table examples include structures such as `ئاو + و + گۆڕ`, `گفت + و + گۆ`, `هێلکە + و + ڕۆن`, `کۆن + ە + فرۆش`, and `خەش + ە + خەش`.

This provides additional secondary support for the broad analysis that Sorani compounds can contain linking material, including patterns written with `ە` and `و`. It also provides independently listed examples such as `گوڵەگەنم` and `ڕەنگاوڕەنگ` that overlap with the owner's inventory.

## 2. What the study does NOT establish
- It is a study of overall derivation, compounding, and borrowing productivity, not a dedicated experimental or distributional study of every proposed interfix.
- Its phrase “linking tool” is broader than a strict morpheme-by-morpheme demonstration that every middle segment is a bound, productive interfix.
- It does not verify the full candidate sets `لە، نە، تا، نا، ەو، او، ان، بە` from the owner source.
- It does not establish that every `و` in an orthographic sequence is a compound-internal interfix rather than a conjunction or part of a linked/open compound.
- It does not establish that every `ە` or `او` sequence in the long owner list has the same segmentation or function.
- The study draws its corpus from dictionaries, not a naturally occurring spoken corpus with speaker-level dialect metadata. Its results should not be presented as proof of productive use across every Central Kurdish variety.

## 3. Important denominator/arithmetic discrepancy
The paper reports a 3,000-word designed corpus (pp. 181–182) and later reports:
- Compounding: 998
- Derivation: 862
- Borrowing: 467
- Total classified in these three categories: 2,327

The paper then reports 43%, 37%, and 20%, respectively. These percentages correspond to the subtotal 2,327, not to the stated 3,000-word corpus:
- 998 / 2,327 = 42.89% ≈ 43%
- 862 / 2,327 = 37.04% ≈ 37%
- 467 / 2,327 = 20.07% ≈ 20%

But 2,327 is 673 words fewer than 3,000. The paper does not explain in the visible report why 673 corpus items are outside these three reported counts. If the denominator were the full 3,000, the corresponding percentages would instead be:
- Compounding: 33.27%
- Derivation: 28.73%
- Borrowing: 15.57%
- Unaccounted in the three reported categories: 22.43%

Therefore the safe statement is: **within the 2,327 items counted under the three reported processes, compounding is the largest category (about 43%)**. Do not say “43% of the full 3,000-word corpus” unless the authors clarify the 673-item difference or provide the complete coding table.

This discrepancy does not invalidate the study's existence or all of its examples; it limits how its numerical productivity result can be cited.

## 4. Productivity criteria that can improve Lesson 3
The authors discuss transparency/expectedness, lexicalization, frequency, and restrictions on productivity, including categorical, phonological, semantic, etymological, syntactic, pragmatic, and blocking factors (pp. 183–185).

For Lesson 3, this suggests a more rigorous checklist before calling a candidate pattern productive:
1. **Segmentation:** are the two bases and middle element independently identifiable?
2. **Category selection:** which types of bases can combine with the element?
3. **Semantic contribution:** is the result predictable, idiomatic, or lexicalized?
4. **Distribution/frequency:** how many independent lexical items attest the pattern, and is it recurring beyond a short list?
5. **Phonological conditioning:** does the form depend on adjacent sounds, stress, or hiatus?
6. **Dialect and register:** which variety and usage register attest it?
7. **Blocking and lexical convention:** do established lexical items prevent or replace an otherwise expected form?

The checklist should be used as a method of analysis, not as a claim that the study has already applied all seven tests to each interfix.

## 6. Required editorial correction to Lesson 3's evidence language
Where Lesson 3 or its appendix describes `-ە-` and `-و-`, cite each source for only the examples and variety it actually discusses:
- Huseen & Shuani (2024): specific `-ە-` and `-و-` interfix examples.
- Jambrović & Hassan (2023): Silemani nominal linkers and compound diagnostics; not evidence for the owner's entire linker inventory.
- Mustafa & Jukil (2025): broader dictionary-based Sorani compounding categories and examples; not a dedicated validation of all interfix segments or all candidate forms.

Do not use the 2025 study's 43% figure as a whole-corpus rate. Record the 673-item denominator discrepancy wherever the result is cited.

## 7. Proposed teaching activity (proposal only)
Give learners five examples drawn from the cited literature and ask them to label:
- compound without a linking tool;
- compound with a linker;
- open/coordinate linked construction;
- phrase with a free conjunction/preposition;
- uncertain lexical candidate.

Require a justification using segmentation, stress/phonological-word behavior, semantic transparency, category restrictions, and attestation. Do not ask them to memorize the unverified `-ەو-/ -او-/ -ان-/ -بە-` lists as rules.
