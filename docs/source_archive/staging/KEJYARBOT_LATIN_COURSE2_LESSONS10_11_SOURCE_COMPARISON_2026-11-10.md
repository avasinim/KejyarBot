## Lesson 10 — Pronavên Bireser û Berbihevîn

### Source-supported
- §86 (p.76) lists the direct-object pronoun forms `min, te, wî, wê, me, we, wan`, and gives examples in which these are direct objects in the basic construction.
- §86 explains the role alternation in past transitive clauses: where the verb agrees with/has a transitive past construction, the pronoun forms switch roles; examples include `min tu dîtî`, `te ez dîtim`, `wî em dîtin`, `wê hon dîtin`, `me tu dîtî`, `we ez dîtim`, and `wan em dîtin`.
- The source explicitly cautions that with indirect objects the pronouns may remain in their familiar forms: `min ji wan re got`, `te ji min stand`, `wî ji wan girt`, etc. The draft correctly distinguishes grammatical role from the surface form.
- §87 (pp.76–77) describes `xwe` as neutral across gender and number, with examples `wî xwe kuşt`, `wê xwe kuşt`, `wan xwe kuşt`, and explains the core same-person reflexive pattern.
- §§89–90 (p.77) support the singular neutral pronoun `ê` and reciprocal forms `hev` and `êk`. The source specifically says `ê` does not replace a plural form such as `wan`.
- §§91–93 (pp.79–80) support the compound pronouns `jê, lê, pê, tê` and `jev/jêk, lev/lêk, pev/pêk, tev/têk`, formed with prepositions and pronouns.
- The draft's warning not to repeat a preposition already contained in a compound pronoun is source-supported: §93 rejects a doubled form like `bi wî pê ve` and gives the compact alternative `pê ve` (or `bi wî ve`).

### Precision notes
1. The draft's explanation of `xwe` focuses on the same-person subject/object reflexive pattern. This is correct as a core rule but incomplete as a full account: §§87–88 (pp.76–77) also discuss cases involving coordinated pronouns and different person/number combinations. Do not state or imply that the core reflexive pattern exhausts all uses of `xwe`.
2. The source defines reciprocal `hev/êk` as pronouns that stand for two or three people together (and can refer to more than two); the draft's phrase “du an zêdetir kes/tişt” is broader than the passage, which specifically describes people. Either keep the source scope or label extension to things as an editorial generalization.
3. The draft's direct-object pronoun inventory is accurate as the core set, but should not be mistaken for every personal-pronoun form in the language. Lesson 9's separate completeness note about `xwe` and `ê` remains relevant.
4. The example `Min qelem ji destê xwe xist` is supported as a reflexive use; its transformed version `Min ew ji destê xwe xist` appears in the source's examples on p.73. Preserve the syntactic distinction when explaining the examples.

## Lesson 11 — Pronavên Îşarkî, Girêkî û Pirsiyarkî

### Source-supported
- §§94–98 (pp.81–82) describe demonstrative pronouns. The two close/far forms are `ev/ew`; the three “qerse” forms are `yê/ya/yên`.
- §96 (p.81) explains that `ev/ew` are declined according to the gender and number of the noun they represent, and that their forms may appear without the written `e` in some inflected contexts. The draft correctly distinguishes their use as pronouns from their use before an overt noun.
- §97 (p.82) states that `yê/ya/yên` refer to people, things, or animals without marking proximity/distance; in their construction they are always subjects, not direct objects, and are followed by the relative pronoun `ko`. The draft's examples are source-based.
- §§99–100 (p.83) support `ko` as a relative pronoun and distinguish it from the conjunction `ko`. The relative pronoun can function as subject or direct object according to the clause: the source gives both `miro vê ko tu dîtî...` and `miro vê ko te dît...`. The conjunction `ko` links clauses and can mean “heke” in conditional contexts.
- §§101–103 (pp.84–85) support interrogative pronouns `kî/kê`, `çi/çî`, and the forms `kîjik/kîjan`. The source distinguishes subject and direct-object uses for `kî/kê` and `çi/çî`.
- §§104–105 (p.85) support `çend` and `kû` as interrogative forms, respectively for quantity and place.
- The draft's distinction between `ko` as a relative pronoun and `ko` as a conjunction is important and directly supported.

### Precision notes
1. Make the relative-pronoun `ko` examples explicit: `Yê ko tu dîtî...` (subject function in the relative clause) versus `Yê ko te dît...` (direct-object function). The draft currently explains the distinction abstractly but does not show the contrast.
2. Keep the rule that `yê/ya/yên` themselves are subjects in their construction separate from the role of the following `ko` inside its own relative clause. These are two different syntactic layers.
3. The possessive-pronoun table in the draft is a selected subset of §§114–117 (pp.87–88). The source includes proximity/farness distinctions in third person, e.g. `yê vî/vê/wî/wê`, `yên vî/vê/wî/wê`, and forms with `van/wan`. The current table's abbreviated `yê wî`, `ya wê`, and `yên wan` examples are not false, but omit the source's close/far contrasts. Add a scope note or include the omitted contrasts in a later completeness pass.
4. The course's use of `kîjan` is source-based; the source also has the variant/related form `kîjik`. The draft mentions both but does not explain their relationship. Do not silently normalize one into the other.
5. The source treats `çend` and `kû` in their own numbered subsections (§§104–105); preserve their distinct functions (quantity vs place) rather than merging them into a generic interrogative list.
