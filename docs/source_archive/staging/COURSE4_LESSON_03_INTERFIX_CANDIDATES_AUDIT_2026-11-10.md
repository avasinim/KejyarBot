## Primary research source: Huseen & Shuani (2024)
Full paper: https://jls.tu.edu.iq/index.php/JLS/article/download/800/897/1617
Article page: https://jls.tu.edu.iq/article_193227.html
Citation: Kocher abdulmajed Huseen & Rafiq Muhammed Mhedeen Shuani (2024), “Interfex according to morphology in Kurdish language”, *Journal of Language Studies*, 8(1), 196–219. DOI: 10.25130/Lang.8.1.9.

The paper identifies its scope as Central Kurdish and explicitly focuses on the interfixes `-و-` and `-ە-` (PDF pp. 197–198; lines 117–170 in the extracted PDF). Its examples and analyses cover multiple patterns of compounds, including:
- `-و-` in coordinate compounds / paired forms and other compound patterns (PDF pp. 207–210; extracted lines 1229–1445).
- `-ە-` in noun/adjective, adjective/noun, noun/verb-root and reduplicative compound patterns (PDF pp. 211–214; extracted lines 1515–1778).

It does NOT provide direct evidence for the specific forms `-لە-`, `-نە-`, `-تا-`, `-نا-`, or `-ەو-` as interfixes. Therefore it must not be cited as proof that those five forms are interfixes.

## Independent source: Jambrović & Hassan (2023)
Full paper: https://twpl.library.utoronto.ca/index.php/twpl/article/download/41669/31972/112597
Article page: https://twpl.library.utoronto.ca/index.php/twpl/article/view/41669
Citation: Samuel Jambrović & Mohammed K. Hassan (2023), “Nominal linkers in Central Kurdish (Silemānī variety)”, *Toronto Working Papers in Linguistics*, 45(1). DOI: 10.33137/twpl.v45i1.41669.

This study distinguishes nominal phrase linkers and compounds in the Silemānī variety. It documents `-ī` and `-a` linkers in noun phrases and discusses `-a` in compounds (abstract and sections 3–4; PDF lines 6–19, 57–66, 924–1009). Examples include `rash-a-bā`, `kar-a-kewī`, `hawr-a-trīshqa`, and `guł-a-bākh`. It does not document `guł-a-bākh` or `kar-a-kewī` as evidence for the forms `لە`, `نە`, `تا`, `نا`, or `ەو`; it is evidence for the distinct linker `-a-` in compounds.

## Correction to the earlier Lesson 3 audit
The earlier audit text stated that Jambrović & Hassan (2023) confirmed both `گوڵەباخ` and `گاکێوی`. The paper actually provides `guł-a-bākh` and `kar-a-kewī` as examples, and attributes them to earlier sources (Abbas 2021: 86; Tahir 2018: 44). The correct transliteration/analysis in this source is therefore `guł-a-bākh` and `kar-a-kewī`; do not treat the paper as directly validating every Kurdish spelling variant or every other form previously grouped with them. This correction supplements the earlier audit without overwriting it.

## Assessment of the newly supplied candidate forms

### 1. `لە` in forms such as `داخلەدڵ`, `شەولەبان`, `خاڵلەڕوو`, `قسەلەڕوو`
- `لە` is also an independently occurring preposition in Sorani syntax. A sequence containing `لە` is not automatically a compound or interfix construction.
- The supplied examples may be lexicalized compounds, fixed expressions, or syntactic phrases with orthographic fusion. The examples alone do not decide between these analyses.
- Before teaching `-لە-` as a productive interfix, each item needs lexical evidence, syntactic behavior, stress/phonological-word evidence, meaning, and speaker/dialect judgments.
- Provisional status: candidate set only; no universal productivity claim.

### 2. `نە` in forms such as `کەسنەزان`, `بارنەبەر`, `چاونەترس`, `تێرنەخۆر`, `گوێنەدەر`
- The sequence `نە` can express negation in Kurdish; its presence between two lexical elements does not by itself establish an interfix.
- These forms may involve negation/derivation, lexicalized compounds, or another word-formation pattern. They require morpheme-boundary and semantic tests before classification.
- Provisional status: do not teach `-نە-` as an interfix based on this list alone.

### 3. `تا` in `سەرتاپا`, `سەرتاپێ`, `سەرتاسەر`
- The supplied contrast between a lexicalized form and the free phrase `لە پێ تا سەر` is pedagogically useful: the same string may occur inside a lexicalized word or as a free adposition/construction.
- The contrast is not enough to establish `-تا-` as a bound interfix. The word `سەرتاپا` and the free sequence `لە پێ تا سەر` must be analyzed separately.
- Provisional status: teach the contrast between lexicalized compounds and free phrases; hold the interfix label pending independent evidence.

### 4. `نا` in `تاوناتاو`, `شەوناشەو`, `جارناجار`, `ڕۆژناڕۆژ`
- The forms appear to involve repetition, temporal/distributive meaning, or lexicalized reduplicative patterns. The inserted string `نا` cannot be labeled an interfix solely because it occurs between repeated-looking elements.
- Each form requires analysis of the base forms, semantic contribution, productivity, stress, and whether the middle element is a bound morpheme or an independent lexical item.
- Provisional status: reduplicative/lexicalized candidates, not a confirmed `-نا-` interfix class.

## Recommended non-destructive teaching treatment
1. Keep the established research-backed discussion of `-ە-` and `-و-` separate from the user's candidate series.
2. Add a short caution box: “A middle element is not automatically an interfix. First test whether it is bound, whether it contributes a stable word-formation function, whether the output is a lexical/phonological word, and whether the pattern is productive.”
3. Add a contrast activity using only secure evidence:
   - identify `-ە-` and `-و-` in the cited compound examples from Huseen & Shuani;
   - distinguish the free phrase `لە پێ تا سەر` from the lexicalized candidate `سەرتاپا`;
   - explain why the latter contrast alone does not prove `-تا-` is an interfix.
4. Keep all examples from the user's `لە/نە/تا/نا` lists in a clearly marked “candidate forms pending verification” appendix, not as settled rules.
