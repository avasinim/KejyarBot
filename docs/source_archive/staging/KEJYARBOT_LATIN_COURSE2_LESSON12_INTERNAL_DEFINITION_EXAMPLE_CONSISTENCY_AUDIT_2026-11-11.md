## 1. Opening definition is too broad for the six-class taxonomy

The definition at Lesson 12 §1 (line 491) says an adjective comes with a noun and identifies its “taybetmendî, nîşan, hejmar, pirs, nebinavkirin an xwedîtiya wê”. This is a useful course-level umbrella, but it groups together several semantic/grammatical relations without explaining the boundary between adjective, determiner, pronoun, and numeral category. The phrase “bi navdêrê re tê” also risks implying that every item in the six groups must always be adjacent to, or overtly accompanied by, a noun.

Teacher-facing clarification:
- State explicitly that the six labels are the taxonomy adopted by this course, not a claim that all grammatical traditions classify demonstratives, interrogatives, quantifiers, and possessive forms as adjectives.
- Distinguish lexical/category label from phrase function and surface position.
- For attributive examples, identify the head noun and the modifier/determiner relation. For independent/substantive use, identify the context and do not automatically retain an attributive label.
- Do not define adjective solely as “a word that describes a noun”; the lesson's own classes include pointing, quantifying, questioning, and possession relations, not only descriptive qualities.

## 2. Section-by-section consistency findings

### §2 — `wesfîn`

The examples `mirovê baş`, `hespê mezin`, and `mehînên spehî` align with the descriptive-quality definition. The degree discussion gives the base form and `-tir` comparative, and separately labels `herî mezin` as a modern pedagogical form rather than a direct example from the cited source. Preserve that source distinction.

Open editorial check: ensure the comparative/superlative terminology is consistently named in the wider course. This audit does not validate every inflectional detail of the listed forms.

### §3 — `îşarkî`

The contrast between `ev mirov` / `ew mirov` and independent `ev/ew` is pedagogically useful: the same surface form may be attributive with an expressed noun or pronominal without one.

The forms `vî hespî / van hespan`, `wî hespî / wan hespan`, `vê mehînê / van mehînan`, and `wê mehînê / wan mehînan` are presented as gender/number/case-conditioned forms. Avoid treating the isolated oblique forms as freely interchangeable dictionary forms. Their precise case environment should be supplied in a full clause if the exercise assesses inflection rather than recognition.

The sentence “navdêr li gorî zayend, mêjer û tewangê dikare biguhere” is broad. The following statement that sometimes only the final noun in a noun group is marked should remain tied to its source construction; do not generalize it to every noun phrase.

### §4 — `jmarîn`

The basic examples `sê mal`, `çar xanî`, and `deh bizin` clearly show a numeral associated with an expressed noun. The paired examples `deh mirov hatin / deh mirovan got` and `çel mirov hatin / çel mirovan got` additionally involve case and clause environment. They should not be used as bare memorization pairs without explaining what licenses the noun form in each clause.

The ordinal examples `stûna çaran`, `a pêncan`, and `ê heftan` show that the course's numeral-adjective discussion includes ordinal forms. Keep `rêzîn` as a subtype, not a seventh top-level class. The reduced forms `a pêncan` and `ê heftan` depend on recoverable nominal context; if used in an exercise, supply that context or teach them explicitly as elliptical forms.

### §5 — `pirsiyarkî`

The examples `kîjan mirov` and `çend kitêb` fit the intended attributive/interrogative pattern. However, `çend mirovan xwar?` is a full clause with an oblique-marked noun; the teacher key should identify the interrogative scope and explain the clause/case environment, not mark the category by surface position alone.

The statement that these forms ask about identification or number is appropriate as a broad course description, but `kîjan` and `çend` should not be treated as semantically identical: one selects among alternatives, the other asks about quantity.

### §6 — `nebinavkirî`

The list mixes quantifiers and indefinite expressions: `tu`, `her`, `herçî`, `filan`, `gelek`, `hemî`, `hevqas`, and `çend`. It is a course-internal grouping; the teacher explanation should distinguish universal, existential/indefinite, and quantity meanings rather than imply they all encode the same kind of indefiniteness.

The note that `gelek`, `hemî`, and `çend` can function pronominally depending on position is important. The same context-sensitivity applies to the assessment: `gelek fêkî` and `çend çiya` are noun-associated examples; a bare `gelek` or `çend` may be independent/substantive.

### §7 — `xwemalîn`

The section groups forms such as `hespê min`, `mehînên te`, `kitêba vî`, `qelemên wê`, `xaniyê me`, `çiyayê we`, `kurên van`, and `keçên wan`. These are all intended by the lesson to express possession/association, but the possessor expressions are not all the same morphological type: personal pronouns and demonstrative forms occur in different shapes and case environments.

The blanket rule “always after the noun, with a veqetandek between them” is too compressed for the mixed examples. In particular, `kitêba vî` should be reviewed against the source's exact analysis: do not automatically teach `vî` as morphologically identical to the personal possessor forms `min/te/me/wê`. Preserve the provisional course label for `kurên van` from the previous rubric, but keep its detailed syntax and idiomaticity open for Kurmanji editor review.

Teacher-facing clarification should separate: (a) course label `xwemalîn`; (b) possessor's lexical form; (c) ezafe/agreement morphology; and (d) the phrase's interpretation. Do not infer all four from the word “possessive” alone.

### §8 — `jmarnav`

The distinction between a numeral modifying an expressed noun (`sê mal`) and a numeral used independently/substantively is central to the lesson. The section's own taxonomy divides `jmarnav` into definite and indefinite groups and lists `nîv`, `yek`, and `hezar` as forms with special inflectional behavior.

Important consistency issue: the section lists `nîv` as a basic `jmarnav` example and says its form remains `nîv` before a numeral or noun; Exercise 1 then gives `nîv roj`. The established course disposition is therefore: classify `nîv` lexically as `jmarnav` in this course's taxonomy, and separately describe its phrase-level quantifying/modifying relation to the expressed noun `roj`. Do not silently relabel it `jmarîn` just because `roj` is overt.

The phrase “jmarnav … wek beşeke serbixwe” must not be misread as saying that a numeral can never occur beside a noun. The taxonomy concerns the word-class distinction adopted by the course; the phrase-level function still needs to be named.

## 4. Suggested teacher-facing model explanations

These are explanatory notes for marking and teaching, not replacements for the lesson text:

1. `ev mirov`: `ev` is used attributively to identify the intended noun `mirov`; without the noun, `ev` may function pronominally.
2. `gelek fêkî`: `gelek` quantifies the expressed noun `fêkî`; in a context where the noun is omitted/recoverable, its use may be independent.
3. `sê mal`: `sê` is the course's numeral-adjective use because it specifies the expressed noun `mal`.
4. `nîv roj`: classify `nîv` as `jmarnav` according to the lesson's lexical taxonomy, while separately noting its quantifying relation to `roj`.
5. `hespê min`: course label `xwemalîn`; identify `min` as the possessor expression and the ezafe link separately. Do not collapse the possessor's lexical class into the course's adjective label.
