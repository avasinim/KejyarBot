## Executive disposition

The terminology chain is broadly recoverable and many cross-references are intentionally aligned, but several boundaries need explicit teaching notes. The principal risk is not spelling drift alone: the same form or string is used at different grammatical levels (word class, phrase function, clause role, inflectional form). Keep those levels distinct and do not force one label to cover all uses.

## 1. Controlled distinctions to preserve

### A. `bireserê navdêrê` vs `bireser` vs `pronavên bireser`

- Lesson 6 (lines 196–223) uses `bireserê navdêrê` for a noun-attached modifier/complement: an element or phrase that specifies or adds information to a noun. It can occur before or after the noun and may be realized by different forms.
- Lessons 9–10 use `kirar` and `bireser` as clause-level grammatical roles; Lesson 10's `pronavên bireser` are pronouns occupying an object position in the relevant constructions.
- Lesson 6 line 211 explicitly warns that the noun-attached modifier is not the same as clause-level `bireser`. Preserve this warning in any future summaries and avoid shortening `bireserê navdêrê` to bare `bireser`.
- Lesson 6 line 213 still calls `ev` in `ev keçik` “`pêşnavdêr/bireser`”. This slash-pair remains underdefined and risks conflating a pre-nominal demonstrative/determiner-like modifier with a complement function. Do not claim `pêşnavdêr` and `bireser` are synonyms. The safer course-internal description is: `ev` is a demonstrative form functioning attributively before the expressed noun `keçik`; the exact technical label `pêşnavdêr` remains unadjudicated pending primary-source/editor confirmation.
- Lesson 12's `rengdêrên îşarkî` supplies a later course taxonomy label for demonstratives used with an expressed noun. It does not retroactively prove that every noun-attached modifier in Lesson 6 is an adjective.

### B. `rengdêr` vs `bireserê navdêrê`

- Lesson 12 treats `rengdêr` as a word-class label in its six-class course taxonomy.
- Lesson 6's `bireserê navdêrê` is a broader syntactic/phrase function that may be realized by more than one word class or phrase type.
- Therefore: an adjective may realize the noun-modifying function, but not every noun-attached modifier/complement is necessarily an adjective. Keep the category and function separate.

### C. `veqetandek` vs `pêvek` vs `tewang`

- Lesson 7 uses `veqetandekên binavkirî` for definiteness-marking forms and explicitly separates that function from inflection/case.
- Lesson 8 uses `veqetandekên nebinavkirî` and distinguishes the basic `-ek/-ek/-in`, pre-adjective `-î/-e/-en`, and oblique forms `-ekî/-ekê/-ina`. The lesson explicitly says these sets are distinct and not interchangeable.
- The earlier terminology decision remains: `veqetandek` names the determiner/definiteness category or function in these lessons; `pêvek` is the broader affix/suffix term. A `veqetandek` is not automatically a suffix in every construction, and a suffix is not automatically a determiner.
- Tewang is a separate inflectional process. Do not teach the definite/indefinite marker itself as synonymous with case marking merely because both may appear on the same noun form.
- Lesson 12 line 578 says a possessive adjective follows the noun and a `veqetandek` intervenes. Because this is a broad statement over mixed possessor forms, it requires the separate Lesson 12 possessive morphology caveat: do not infer that every possessor form has identical morphology or that all intervening elements are interchangeable with the Lesson 7/8 determiner suffix paradigms.

### D. `pronav` vs `rengdêr` and context-dependent forms

- Lessons 10–11 distinguish pronouns by their independent/replacement function, and Lesson 12 distinguishes attributive adjective use from independent pronoun use for forms such as `ev/ew`.
- The course's examples support a context/function test: `ev keçik` (attributive demonstrative under Lesson 12's taxonomy) vs `Ev hat.` (independent demonstrative pronoun); `hespê min` (possessive modifier with expressed noun) vs `yê min/yên min` (possessive pronoun replacing a recoverable noun/group).
- Lesson 11 also treats `çend` as interrogative pronoun when used alone and discusses its noun-associated interrogative use; Lesson 12 places `çend` among indefinite adjectives in one section. This is not necessarily a contradiction if the use is explicitly distinguished, but the course should avoid presenting a bare lexical form as having one invariant class regardless of syntax.
- Lesson 12 similarly notes `gelek` and `hemî` can be pronominal depending on sentence position. Keep this contextual warning visible in teaching and assessment.

### E. `jmarîn` vs `jmarnav`

- Lesson 12 is the first explicit course taxonomy that separates numeral-adjective use with an expressed noun (`sê mal`, `stûna çaran`) from the independent/substantive numeral category `jmarnav`.
- Do not retroactively reclassify every numeral mentioned in earlier lessons as `rengdêr` or `jmarnav` without checking its syntactic use.
- Preserve the existing Lesson 12 disposition for `nîv roj`: lexical course label `jmarnav`; phrase-level function quantifies/modifies the expressed noun `roj`.
- `rêzîn` remains a subtype within `jmarîn` in the six-class course framework, not a seventh adjective class. Its occurrence in the numeral-pronoun section also requires the context/function distinction to be stated.

### F. `kirar` vs `kiryar`

- Lessons 9–10 use `kirar` for the subject/agent grammatical role and carefully distinguish the pronoun's morphological `forma kirarî` from its actual clause role.
- Lesson 12 quiz Q1 contains `kiryarê`, while the controlled register has `kirar`. This is a live spelling/lemma/inflection query, not a resolved synonym. Do not silently replace one with the other or substitute `kirde` without source/editor adjudication.

## 3. Priority corrections for a future authorized teaching revision

1. Replace the slash construction `pêşnavdêr/bireser` only after a Kurmanji editor/source ruling; meanwhile add a teacher note that identifies the attributive demonstrative function without asserting synonymy.
2. Keep Lesson 6's explicit distinction between noun-attached modifier and clause-level object visible when Lesson 10 introduces object pronouns.
3. Add a cross-reference from Lessons 7–8 to Lesson 12's possessive section explaining that the same word `veqetandek` should not cause learners to merge determiner morphology with possessive/ezafe morphology.
4. When introducing Lesson 12, recap the difference between word class and syntactic function, with paired examples from Lessons 6, 10, and 11.
5. Keep context-sensitive forms (`ev/ew`, `çend`, `gelek`, `hemî`) in full phrases/sentences for assessment.
6. Resolve `kiryar/kirar` and `rengdêra/rengdêrê jmarîn` only with a qualified Kurmanji editor and explicit evidence.
